The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the high water and methane conditions, while the system controls the pump.

Initially, the environment is assumed to be in a safe state, with no high water and no methane present. In this initial state, the pump is required to be off.

The system must behave according to the following rules: if the water is high, the pump must not be on at the current moment, but it may be turned on at the next moment. Additionally, if methane is detected, the system must ensure that if the water is high, the pump will be turned off at the next moment.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was on in the previous moment and is still on in the current moment, the water level will decrease, moving towards a state where there is no high water. Furthermore, the environment is assumed to maintain a consistent state regarding the water level, meaning that if there is no high water, it will either remain that way or the presence of methane will be detected.

The system's operation is guided by these assumptions about the environment's behavior, ensuring the pump's operation is safe and effective under the given conditions.
