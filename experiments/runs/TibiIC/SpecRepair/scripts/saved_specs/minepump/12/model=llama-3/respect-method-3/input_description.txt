The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: high water levels and the presence of methane. The environment controls the detection of these conditions, while the system controls the activation of a pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. Under these initial conditions, the pump should be off.

The system is required to behave as follows: if high water is detected, then the pump should be activated at the next opportunity, as long as high water conditions persist. Additionally, if methane is detected, the system must ensure that the pump is turned off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is currently on, then at the next moment, the water level should not be high. Furthermore, it is assumed that if the pump is on, then either the water level is not high or there is no methane present.

In summary, the system must control the pump based on the environmental conditions of high water and methane detection, while the environment must adhere to certain constraints regarding the relationships between the pump's state and these conditions. The system's primary objective is to manage the pump's state to respond appropriately to the environmental conditions, ensuring safe operation.
