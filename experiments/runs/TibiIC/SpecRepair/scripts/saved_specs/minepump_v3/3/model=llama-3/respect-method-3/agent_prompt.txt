The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: high water levels and the presence of methane. The environment controls the detection of these conditions, while the system controls the activation of a pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. In this initial state, the pump is not activated.

The system guarantees that if high water is detected, the pump will be activated at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, the system ensures that either the methane remains present or the pump is deactivated at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump has been active and continues to be active, the high water level will be resolved at the next opportunity. Furthermore, it is assumed that if there is no high water, then either there will continue to be no high water or there will be no methane present.

In summary, the system is designed to respond to environmental conditions by activating the pump when necessary, while the environment is expected to behave in a manner that allows the system to effectively manage the high water and methane levels.
