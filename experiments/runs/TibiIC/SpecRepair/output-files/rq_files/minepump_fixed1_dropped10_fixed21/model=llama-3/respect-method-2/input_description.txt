The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the detection of these conditions, specifically whether there is high water and whether methane is present. The system, in turn, controls a pump.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump is currently off, it will remain off, at least from the environment's perspective in terms of its influence on the system's next state.

The system must guarantee two primary behaviors in response to the environmental conditions. Firstly, if methane is detected, the system must ensure that the pump is turned on at the next opportunity. Secondly, if no methane is detected, the system must ensure that the pump is turned off at the next opportunity.

These guarantees imply that the system's operation of the pump is directly tied to the detection of methane, with the pump's state being updated based on the presence or absence of methane in a way that ensures safety and proper functioning under the given environmental conditions.
