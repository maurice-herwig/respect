The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: high water levels and the presence of methane. The environment controls the detection of these conditions, which are indicated by the highwater and methane signals. In response, the system controls a pump.

The system is expected to operate under the assumption that if the pump is currently off, the environment will not turn it on in the next step unless it was already off in the previous step.

The system must guarantee two main behaviors. Firstly, at any point, the pump must either remain off or be turned on in the next step. This means the system must always have a plan for what to do with the pump in the next step, given its current state.

Secondly, if methane is detected, the system guarantees that the pump will be turned off in the next step. This ensures a safe response to the presence of methane by stopping the pump.

These guarantees must hold at all times, ensuring the system responds appropriately to environmental conditions while adhering to its operational assumptions.
