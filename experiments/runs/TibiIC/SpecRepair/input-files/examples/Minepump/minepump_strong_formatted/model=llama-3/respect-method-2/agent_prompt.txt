The Minepump system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of high water and methane. The system controls the state of a pump. 

Initially, the environment is assumed to have no high water and no methane, and the system's pump is off.

The system guarantees that if high water is present, it will turn on the pump at the next step. Conversely, if methane is detected, the system will turn off the pump at the next step.

The environment is assumed to behave in such a way that if the pump was on in the previous step and is still on, then the water level will be low at the next step. Additionally, the environment is assumed to always be in a state where either there is no high water or there is no methane.

In summary, the system's behavior is defined by its response to the environment's conditions: it must turn on the pump when there is high water and turn it off when there is methane, under the given environmental assumptions. The environment's conditions and the system's responses are intertwined, with the system reacting to the environment's state and the environment's state potentially changing in response to the system's actions.
