The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls these conditions, which are represented by the variables highwater and methane. The system, in turn, controls two outputs: a pump and a flag.

Initially, the environment is assumed to be in a safe state, with no high water and no methane present. In this initial state, the system's pump is required to be off.

The system is designed to respond to changes in the environment. If high water is detected and there is no methane, the system must turn on the pump at the next opportunity. Conversely, if methane is detected, the system must turn off the pump at the next opportunity.

The environment's behavior is also constrained by assumptions. For instance, if the pump is on and was on previously, the environment is expected to ensure that the high water condition is resolved at the next opportunity. Additionally, if high water is present and the pump is not on (either because it was not on previously or it is not on currently), the environment is assumed to persist in the high water state at the next opportunity. The environment is also assumed to eventually have periods where methane is not present and will remain absent at the next opportunity.

The system guarantees certain behaviors. It ensures that the flag will eventually be reset. If high water is detected, the system sets the flag. The system's operation is thus tightly coupled with the environmental conditions, responding to high water and methane levels while ensuring the pump and flag are managed appropriately to handle these conditions safely.
