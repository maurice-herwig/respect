The system, GridA3, interacts with an environment that controls the coordinates of a robot, represented by `rob`, which is a pair of integers between 0 and 5. The system controls the movement of the robot, which can be in one of five directions: up, down, left, right, or stay.

The environment is assumed to move the robot in a way that is consistent with the specified movement directions. Specifically, when the system moves the robot up, the environment must decrease the robot's y-coordinate by 1 and keep the x-coordinate the same. When the system moves the robot down, the environment must increase the robot's y-coordinate by 1 and keep the x-coordinate the same. Similarly, when the system moves the robot left or right, the environment must decrease or increase the robot's x-coordinate by 1, respectively, and keep the y-coordinate the same.

The system guarantees that whenever the robot is at the initial position (0,0), it will eventually move to the final position (5,5). Additionally, the system ensures that the robot will infinitely often be at the initial position (0,0).

Initially, there are no specific constraints on the robot's position. However, the system's safety requirement is that whenever the robot is at the initial position (0,0), it will move to the final position (5,5) at the next step. The liveness requirement is that the robot will repeatedly return to the initial position (0,0) over time. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the robot's coordinates, and the system-controlled output is the movement direction of the robot.
