The Patrolling system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment to control the movement of a robot within a defined area. The environment provides the locations of three targets, A, B, and C, which are fixed and do not change over time. The system controls the robot's position, defined by its x and y coordinates, robotX and robotY, within a grid of size DX by DY.

The environment is assumed to keep the locations of targets A, B, and C constant, meaning that once set, these locations do not change. 

The system guarantees that the robot will infinitely often visit each of the targets A, B, and C. This means that the robot will periodically be at the location of target A, target B, and target C, ensuring that it patrols all three targets over time. 

The robot's movement is constrained within the defined grid, with its position at any given time determined by its current x and y coordinates. The system's objective is to ensure the robot's continuous patrolling of the three targets, without any requirement for the robot to follow a specific path or schedule between visits.
