The Patrolling system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment to control the movement of a robot within a defined grid. The environment provides the locations of three targets, A, B, and C, which are expected to remain stationary. The system controls the robot's position, defined by its x and y coordinates, within the grid.

The environment is assumed to keep the targets at their respective locations, meaning that the x and y coordinates of each target do not change over time.

The system guarantees that the robot starts at the top-left corner of the grid. Additionally, the system must ensure that the robot visits each target (A, B, and C) and the top-left corner infinitely often.

At all times, the robot can move one step in either the x or y direction, or remain stationary. This means that the robot's x and y coordinates can either increase by 1, decrease by 1, or remain the same at each time step.

The environment-controlled inputs are the x and y coordinates of targets A, B, and C. The system-controlled outputs are the x and y coordinates of the robot.
