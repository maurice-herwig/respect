The system, known as CatchingIntruder, involves an ongoing interaction between the environment and the system. The environment controls the position of an intruder, specified by its x and y coordinates (intruderX and intruderY), while the system controls the positions of two robots, with each robot's position specified by its x and y coordinates (robotX0, robotY0 for the first robot and robotX1, robotY1 for the second robot).

The environment is assumed to move the intruder in a way that either increases, decreases, or keeps its x and y coordinates the same by 1 unit at a time, with at least one of the coordinates remaining stationary. This assumption applies at all times.

The system must ensure that each robot moves in a similar manner, with either its x or y coordinate (but not necessarily both) changing by 1 unit or remaining the same at each step. This movement pattern must hold true at all times for both robots.

Initially, the first robot must be positioned at coordinates (0,0), and the second robot must be positioned at coordinates (7,0). The intruder's initial y-coordinate is assumed to be 7, with no restrictions on its initial x-coordinate.

Overall, the system's goal is to control the robots' movements according to these rules, given the environment's control over the intruder's movements and initial positions.
