The Patrolling system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment to control the movement of a robot within a grid. The environment provides the locations of three target goals, while the system controls the robot's position.

The environment-controlled inputs are the x and y coordinates of the three target goals, represented as arrays of integers. The system-controlled outputs are the robot's current x and y coordinates.

The environment is assumed to keep the target goals stationary, meaning their locations do not change over time.

The system guarantees that the robot moves in a way that changes its x and y coordinates by at most one unit at a time. Additionally, the system ensures that the robot will infinitely often be in the same location as each of the three target goals.

There are no specified initial conditions for the robot's position or the target goals' locations. The system's safety requirement is that the robot's movement is restricted to changing its coordinates by at most one unit at a time, in both the x and y directions. The liveness requirements are that the robot will eventually visit each of the three target goals infinitely often. 

The next-state relationships are defined such that the robot's x and y coordinates can either increase by one, decrease by one, or remain the same in each time step.
