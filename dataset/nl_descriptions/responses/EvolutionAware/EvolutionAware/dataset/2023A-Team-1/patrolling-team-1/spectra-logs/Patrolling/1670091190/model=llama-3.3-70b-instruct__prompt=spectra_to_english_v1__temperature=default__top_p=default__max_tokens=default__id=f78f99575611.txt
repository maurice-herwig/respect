The Patrolling system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment to control the movement of a robot within a defined area. The environment provides the locations of three targets, represented by their x and y coordinates, which are stored in the `goalsX` and `goalsY` arrays. The system controls the robot's position, represented by its x and y coordinates, stored in `robotX` and `robotY`.

The environment is assumed to keep the target locations fixed, meaning that the x and y coordinates of each target do not change over time.

The system guarantees that the robot moves in a way that its x and y coordinates change by at most 1 unit at a time, allowing it to move horizontally, vertically, or stay in place. 

Additionally, the system ensures that the robot visits each target location infinitely often. In other words, the robot will eventually be in the same position as each target, as defined by the `inPlace` predicate, which checks if the robot's x and y coordinates match those of a target. This visiting behavior is required to happen infinitely often for all three targets. 

The system's behavior is defined within a grid of size 4x4, where the x and y coordinates of the robot and targets are restricted to the range 0 to 4 (DX and DY). 

Initially, the robot's position is not specified, and its movement starts from an arbitrary location within the defined grid. The system's movement and visiting behavior are the primary requirements, and the robot's position is updated based on the allowed movements, which are restricted to changing its x and y coordinates by at most 1 unit at a time.
