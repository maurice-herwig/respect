The Patrolling system is a reactive system that interacts with an environment to control a robot's movement. The environment controls the positions and colors of three targets, A, B, and C, while the system controls the robot's position. The targets' positions are defined by their x and y coordinates (targetAX, targetAY, targetBX, targetBY, targetCX, targetCY), and their colors are represented by boolean values indicating whether they are green (Agreen, Bgreen, Cgreen).

The system guarantees that the robot will eventually visit each target when it is green. Specifically, the robot will infinitely often be at target A, target B, and target C. Additionally, the system ensures that the robot will never visit a target when it is red.

The system's behavior is constrained by the environment's control over the targets' positions and colors. The environment determines when and where the targets are green or red, and the system responds by navigating the robot to the targets accordingly.

The robot's position is defined by its x and y coordinates (robotX, robotY), which are updated by the system to achieve the guaranteed behavior. The system's goal is to visit each target when it is green, while avoiding targets when they are red, and to do so in a way that satisfies the liveness requirements of eventually visiting each target. 

Initially, the robot's position and the targets' positions and colors are not specified, and the system's behavior begins from an unspecified state. The system's safety requirements ensure that the robot never visits a red target, and its liveness requirements ensure that the robot eventually visits each green target.
