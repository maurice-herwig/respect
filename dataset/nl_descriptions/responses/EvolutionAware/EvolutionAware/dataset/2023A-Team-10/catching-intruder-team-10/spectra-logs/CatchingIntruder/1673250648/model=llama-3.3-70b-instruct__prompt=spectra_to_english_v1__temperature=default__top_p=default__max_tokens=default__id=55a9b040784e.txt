The system, called CatchingIntruder, interacts with its environment in a reactive manner. The system controls four outputs: the initial x and y coordinates of two robots, denoted as robotX0, robotY0, robotX1, and robotY1, which are all within a defined dimension of 0 to 7.

The environment is assumed to provide the next values for the robots' positions. The system guarantees that the robots will never be at certain obstacle locations, specifically (2,1), (3,1), (4,1), and (5,1), for both robots. Additionally, the system ensures that the two robots will never collide with each other, meaning they will never occupy the same position at the same time.

The system also guarantees that whenever a robot moves in one dimension, its position in the other dimension will be updated to the next value. For example, if the robot moves in the x dimension, its y position will be updated, and vice versa. The movement in a dimension is defined as either incrementing or decrementing the current position by 1.

At all times, the system must satisfy these safety conditions: avoiding obstacles and collisions, and updating the robots' positions according to the movement rules. The system's behavior is defined by these guarantees, ensuring a safe and coordinated movement of the two robots within the defined space.
