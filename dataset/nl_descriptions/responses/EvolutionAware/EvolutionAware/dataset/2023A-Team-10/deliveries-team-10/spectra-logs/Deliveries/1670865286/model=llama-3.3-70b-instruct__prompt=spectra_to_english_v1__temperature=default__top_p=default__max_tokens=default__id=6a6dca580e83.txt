The system, called Deliveries, interacts with an environment to control the movement of two robots within a defined grid. The environment controls the vertical position of an elevator, while the system controls the horizontal and vertical positions of the two robots. 

Initially, both robots are positioned at a vertical level of 0. The system must ensure that the robots never collide, meaning they are never at the same horizontal and vertical position simultaneously. 

At all times, both robots must move horizontally, either staying in place, moving one position to the left, or moving one position to the right. When a robot is aligned with the elevator's horizontal position (which is fixed at 0), its vertical position must match the elevator's vertical position, which is controlled by the environment. 

The environment's behavior is not restricted by any explicit assumptions, but its actions indirectly affect the system through the elevator's vertical position. The system's guarantees focus on maintaining safe and coordinated movement of the robots in response to the environment's input. 

The system does not have any liveness requirements that necessitate specific events to occur infinitely often, but rather it must continuously satisfy the safety conditions of non-collision and coordinated movement with the elevator. The next-state relationships for the robots' positions are defined by the predicates for horizontal movement, allowing for flexible and dynamic adjustments based on the current positions.
