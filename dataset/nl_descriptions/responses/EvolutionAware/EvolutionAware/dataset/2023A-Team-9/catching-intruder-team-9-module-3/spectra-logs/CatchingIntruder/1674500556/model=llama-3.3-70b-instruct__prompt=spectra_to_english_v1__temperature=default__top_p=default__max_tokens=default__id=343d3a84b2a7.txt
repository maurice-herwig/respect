The system, called CatchingIntruder, describes an interaction between the environment, which controls an intruder's position, and the system, which controls the positions of two robots. The environment's inputs are the intruder's x and y coordinates, denoted as intruderX and intruderY, while the system's outputs are the x and y coordinates of two robots, denoted as robotX0, robotY0, robotX1, and robotY1.

The environment is assumed to behave in a specific way. The intruder must not coincide with either robot at any time. Additionally, the intruder's movements are restricted such that it can only move to an adjacent cell if that cell is clear of both robots and certain predefined obstacles.

The system guarantees that the intruder will always have a valid move available, either up, down, left, or right, as long as the destination cell is clear of both robots and the predefined obstacles. The intruder's movement is restricted to adjacent cells, and its position can change by either moving one step in any of the four main directions or staying in the same position.

The system's behavior is defined within a 7x7 grid, where each cell is identified by its x and y coordinates, ranging from 0 to 7. Certain cells, specifically (2,1), (3,1), (4,1), and (5,1), are considered obstacles and are always clear of the robots but restricted for the intruder's movement. 

There are no specified initial conditions or liveness requirements for the system beyond the safety requirements imposed by the environment's assumptions and the system's guarantees. The system's primary objective is to ensure that the intruder always has a valid move, given the constraints on the environment's behavior.
