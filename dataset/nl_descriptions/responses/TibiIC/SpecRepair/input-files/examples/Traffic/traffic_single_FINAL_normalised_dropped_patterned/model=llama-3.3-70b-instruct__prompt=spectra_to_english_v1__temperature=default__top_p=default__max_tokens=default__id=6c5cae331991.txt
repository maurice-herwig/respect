The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal. 

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it is expected that police will not be present indefinitely, and there will be times when there is no car. Additionally, the car will not start moving when the light is red unless there is an emergency or the car is already moving, and the car will not remain stationary when the light is green unless there is a police presence or the car is about to move.

The system guarantees two main behaviors: the green light will appear frequently in response to the presence of a car, and there will be times when there is no car. The system's response to the car's presence is defined by a pattern that ensures the green light is displayed when a car is present, except initially or when the car is not present. This pattern has two states, and the system switches between these states based on the presence of the car and the green light. Initially, the system starts in a state where the green light is displayed when a car is present. The system then updates its state based on the current state, the presence of the car, and the green light, ensuring that the green light is displayed frequently when a car is present. The system will return to its initial state when the green light is displayed after a car has been present. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence, while the system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, indicated by the green signal.
