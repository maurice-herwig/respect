The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The system controls a pump. 

Initially, the environment is assumed to have no high water and no methane present, and the system's pump is off.

The environment is expected to behave in such a way that if the pump was previously on and is currently on, then the high water condition will be resolved in the next state. Additionally, the environment is assumed to maintain a condition where if there is no high water, then either there will still be no high water or there will be no methane in the next state.

The system must guarantee that if high water is present, it will continue to be present until the pump is turned on. Furthermore, if methane is detected, the system must turn off the pump in the next state.

In summary, the environment-controlled inputs are the high water and methane conditions, and the system-controlled output is the state of the pump. The system's behavior is constrained by the need to respond to the environment's conditions while ensuring the pump operates correctly to mitigate the high water and methane conditions.
