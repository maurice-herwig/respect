The Minepump system operates in an environment with two controlled inputs: highwater and methane, which indicate the presence of high water and methane, respectively. The system controls one output: pump, which represents the state of a pump.

Initially, the environment is assumed to be in a safe state, with no high water and no methane present. Correspondingly, the system's pump is initially off.

The system guarantees that if high water is detected, it will turn on the pump at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, the system will turn off the pump at the next opportunity, unless high water is also present.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is currently on, the water level will decrease at the next opportunity. Furthermore, the environment is assumed to ensure that if the pump is on, either the water level is not high or there is no methane present.

In summary, the system's behavior is defined by its response to the environment's inputs, with the goal of safely controlling the pump based on the presence of high water and methane. The environment's assumptions restrict its allowed behavior, ensuring that the system's operation is reasonable and effective.
