The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: high water levels and the presence of methane. The environment controls the detection of these conditions, indicated by the variables "highwater" and "methane". The system controls a pump, which can be either on or off, indicated by the variable "pump".

Initially, the environment is assumed to have normal conditions, with no high water and no methane present. Correspondingly, the system is required to have the pump off at the start.

The system must guarantee that if high water is detected, the pump will be off initially but will turn on at the next opportunity. Conversely, if methane is detected, the pump must be on, but it can turn off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump has been on and remains on, the water level will decrease, meaning high water will no longer be present at the next moment. Additionally, the environment is assumed to maintain a state where if there is no high water, then either there will still be no high water or there will be no methane at the next moment.

These conditions outline the expected interaction between the Minepump system and its environment, ensuring safe operation based on the detected conditions and the system's response to them.
