The **Minepump** system manages a pump in response to water levels and methane gas detection, ensuring safe operation under defined conditions. Here’s how it behaves:

### Environment and System Variables
The environment controls two inputs:
- **High water level (`highwater`):** Indicates whether the water level is dangerously high.
- **Methane presence (`methane`):** Indicates whether methane gas is detected.

The system controls one output:
- **Pump (`pump`):** Determines whether the pump is active (`true`) or inactive (`false`).

### Initial Conditions
At startup:
- The environment begins with both `highwater` and `methane` **false** (no high water or methane).
- The system starts with the pump **off** (`pump=false`).

### Assumptions on the Environment
The environment’s behavior is restricted as follows:
1. If the pump has been **continuously running** (*pump was true in the previous state and remains true in the current state*), the water level **must eventually drop** (`highwater` becomes `false` in the next state).
2. If there is **no high water** (`highwater=false`), then either:
   - The water level remains safe (`highwater` stays `false`), or
   - No methane is detected (`methane=false`).

### System Guarantees
The system must satisfy these requirements at all times:
1. **Pump activation for high water:**
   If `highwater` becomes `true`, then either:
   - Methane is detected (`methane=true`)—preventing the pump from turning on, or
   - The pump **must turn on** (`next(pump=true)`) in the next state.

2. **Pump deactivation for methane:**
   If `methane` becomes `true`, then either:
   - The pump is already off (`pump=false`), or
   - The pump **must turn off** (`next(pump=false)`) in the next state.

The system enforces these rules indefinitely to ensure safe operation.
