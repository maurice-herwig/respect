The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the levels of high water and methane, while the system controls the operation of a pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. In this initial state, the pump is guaranteed to be off.

The system guarantees that if high water is present, the pump will be turned on at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, then either high water must be present or the pump must be turned off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is still on, then the high water level will be lowered at the next opportunity. It is also assumed that if the pump is on, then either the high water level must be lowered or the methane level must be lowered.

In summary, the system's purpose is to control the pump based on the levels of high water and methane in the environment, with the goal of safely managing these conditions. The environment's behavior is constrained by assumptions that ensure the system can effectively respond to changes in the high water and methane levels.
