The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls these conditions, while the system controls the state of a pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present, and the pump is off. 

The system must ensure that if high water is present, it will continue to be present until the pump is turned on. Additionally, whenever methane is detected, the pump must be turned off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in such a way that if the pump was previously on and is still on, the water level will decrease at the next step. It is also assumed that if there is no high water, then either there is no high water or there is no methane at the next step.

In summary, the system's goal is to control the pump based on the environmental conditions of high water and methane, while the environment's behavior is constrained by certain assumptions regarding the relationships between these conditions and the pump's state. The environment-controlled inputs are the high water and methane conditions, and the system-controlled output is the state of the pump.
