The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls these conditions, which are represented by the variables highwater and methane. The system, in response, controls a pump, which is either active or inactive.

The environment is assumed to behave in such a way that if the pump is active, it will not cause the water level to rise, meaning that if the pump is on, the water will not become high in the next state.

The system must guarantee two conditions. Firstly, if methane is present, or if the pump will be active in the next state, or if the water is not high, then the system must ensure that this combined condition always holds. Secondly, if methane is not present, then the system must ensure that the pump will not be active in the next state.

In summary, the system must ensure safe operation of the pump based on the environmental conditions of water level and methane presence, under the assumption that active pumping does not lead to high water levels. The environment-controlled inputs are the highwater and methane conditions, and the system-controlled output is the state of the pump.
