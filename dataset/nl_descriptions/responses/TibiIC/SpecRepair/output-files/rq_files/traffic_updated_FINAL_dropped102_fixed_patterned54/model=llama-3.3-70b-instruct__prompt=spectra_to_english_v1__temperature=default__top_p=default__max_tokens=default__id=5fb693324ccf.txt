The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

Similarly, when the light is red for road B, a car will be present on road B only if an emergency is occurring or if the light for road B is green and no car is present on road B. Additionally, when the light is green for road A, a car will not be present on road A unless an emergency is occurring and the light for road A is red, or unless the car was present in the previous state and is now moving. The same logic applies to road B.

When a car is present on road A and the light for road B is red, the system will eventually turn the light for road A green. When a car is present on road B and the light for road B is green, the system will keep the light for road B green. During an emergency, both lights will be red.

The system's response to the presence of cars and emergencies follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present on a road and the corresponding light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system will return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system responds to the presence of cars in a timely and safe manner.
