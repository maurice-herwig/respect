The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by `cara` and `carb`, and the occurrence of emergencies. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, represented by `greena` and `greenb`.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when an emergency is in effect, if the traffic light for road A is red, then there must be no car on road A; similarly, when an emergency is in effect, if the traffic light for road B is red, then the emergency must still be in effect. Additionally, when an emergency is in effect or there is no car on road A, or the traffic light for road A is red, then the car on road A must move or not be present in the next state; a similar assumption applies to road B.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green; similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road B green.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: when a car is present on a road and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green; once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the traffic lights respond to the presence of cars on the roads.
