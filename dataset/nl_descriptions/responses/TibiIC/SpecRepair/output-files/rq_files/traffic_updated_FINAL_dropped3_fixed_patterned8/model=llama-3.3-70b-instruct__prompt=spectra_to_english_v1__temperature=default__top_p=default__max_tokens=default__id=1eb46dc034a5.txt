The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an `emergency` signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road A is red, then there should be no car on road A; similarly, when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road B is red, then the emergency signal should still be active, implying that the system should not have both lights red at the same time when the emergency signal is active. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A should move or the light for road A should be red, and the car on road A should not remain stationary when the light is green. The same logic applies to road B, where the car should move when the light is green.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road A green. The same guarantee applies to road B, where the presence of a car will lead to the light for road B being turned green. 

The system's response to the presence of cars is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will respond to a car's presence by turning the corresponding light green. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of a car. The system remains in this state as long as there is no car present or the light is already green. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. The system will infinitely often return to its initial state, ensuring that it will continue to respond to the presence of cars.
