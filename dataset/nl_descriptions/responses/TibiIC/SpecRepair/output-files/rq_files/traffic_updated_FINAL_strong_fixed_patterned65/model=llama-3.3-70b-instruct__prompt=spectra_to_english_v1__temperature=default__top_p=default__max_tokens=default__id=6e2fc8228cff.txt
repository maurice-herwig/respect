The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next state. When car A is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, both lights will turn red. 

The system's response to car presence is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will respond to the presence of a car by turning its corresponding light green. This pattern has an initial state where the system is not responding to the car's presence. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is already green. If the car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the car's light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. The system will infinitely often be in its initial state, ensuring that it will always respond to the car's presence.
