The TrafficE2 system controls a traffic light, interacting with its environment, which includes the presence of a car, an emergency situation, and police intervention. The environment controls the inputs: whether a car is present, whether an emergency is occurring, and whether the police are present. The system controls the output: whether the traffic light is green.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, it is assumed that the police will not be present indefinitely, and there will be times when they are not present. Additionally, when the traffic light is not green (i.e., red), if the police are not present and there is no emergency, the car will not start moving. However, when the police are present or the car is already moving, the car may start moving even when the traffic light is red. It is also assumed that when the police are present, the car will either not be moving or will stop moving.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic light will turn green often enough to allow the car to pass when it is present. Furthermore, there will be times when there is no car present.

The system's behavior is also constrained by a specific pattern of response to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the car's presence. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system will return to its initial state, allowing the car to pass. If the light remains green, the system will stay in its initial state. If the car is not present or the light is green when a car arrives, the system will remain in its initial state. The system will always eventually return to its initial state, ensuring that it can respond to the car's presence.
