The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally expected that there will be no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red and there is no emergency, the environment will keep car A present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red, the environment will keep car B present in the next state. On the other hand, when car A or car B is present and its corresponding light is green, the environment will move the car, making it absent in the next state.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. If car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. Similarly, if car B is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. When an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

The system's response to the presence of cars is governed by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually respond to the presence of a car by turning its corresponding light green. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the presence of a car. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next presence of a car. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial state, where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars.
