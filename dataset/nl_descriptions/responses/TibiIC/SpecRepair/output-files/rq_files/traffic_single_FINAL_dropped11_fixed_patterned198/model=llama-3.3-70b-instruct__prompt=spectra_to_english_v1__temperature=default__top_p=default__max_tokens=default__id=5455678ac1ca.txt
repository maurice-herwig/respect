The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways: it should not have police interventions frequently, and when a car is present, it should either be an emergency, the traffic light should be green, or the car should not be moving towards the traffic light. Additionally, when a car is not moving, it should either be because there is a police intervention, the car is not present, or the traffic light is not green.

The system guarantees that it will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is defined by a pattern where the system initially waits for a car to arrive. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it waits for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car arrival. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial waiting state, allowing it to respond to cars as they arrive.

The system's initial condition is that it starts in the waiting state. The safety requirements dictate that the system should only transition between its waiting and waiting-for-green states based on the presence of a car and the state of the traffic light. The liveness requirement is that the system will infinitely often return to its waiting state, ensuring that it can respond to cars as they arrive. The next-state relationships define how the system transitions between its states based on the presence of a car and the state of the traffic light.
