The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways. Specifically, when the car is not present, it will either remain absent or become present only when the traffic light is green. Additionally, when the police are present, the car will either be absent or will not be present in the next time step, unless the traffic light is not green.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This is achieved through a state machine that tracks the system's response to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or the traffic light is green when the car is present. If the car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial state, indicating that it has responded to the car's presence.

The system-controlled output is the green traffic light signal, while the environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system's guarantee of responding to the car's presence by turning the traffic light green is a liveness requirement, ensuring that this behavior will happen infinitely often. The system's state machine and the environment's assumptions work together to ensure safe and responsive behavior.
