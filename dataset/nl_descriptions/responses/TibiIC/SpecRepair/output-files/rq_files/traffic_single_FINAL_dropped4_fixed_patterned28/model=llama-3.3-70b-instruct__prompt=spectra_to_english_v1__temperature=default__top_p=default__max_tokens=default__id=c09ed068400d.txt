The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways: it should not have police interventions frequently, and when a car is present, it should either move or be in an emergency situation, or remain idle, whenever the traffic light is not green. Additionally, the environment should ensure that a car is either not present or not moving when the traffic light is not green.

The system guarantees that it will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is characterized by a pattern where the system initially is in a state waiting for a car (S0). When a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state (S1) where it waits for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial waiting state (S0). The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial waiting state (S0), indicating that it periodically responds to the presence of cars by turning the traffic light green. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, specifically the green signal. The system's behavior is constrained by the environment's assumptions, ensuring a coordinated interaction between the environment and the system.
