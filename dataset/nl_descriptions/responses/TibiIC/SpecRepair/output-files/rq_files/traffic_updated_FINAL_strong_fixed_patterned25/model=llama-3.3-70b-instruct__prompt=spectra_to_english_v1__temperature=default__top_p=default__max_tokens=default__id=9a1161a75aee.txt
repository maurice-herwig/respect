The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to the presence of these cars and potential emergencies. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and whether an emergency is occurring. The system controls the traffic lights for car A and car B, denoted as green A and green B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it will not have emergencies often, meaning there will be times when no emergency is present. When car A is present and its light is red and there is no emergency, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red, car B will remain present in the next state. Conversely, when car A or car B is present and its respective light is green, the car will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees several behaviors: the lights for car A and car B will never both be red at the same time. If car A is present, the system will eventually respond by turning its light green. The same guarantee applies to car B. During an emergency, both lights will be red.

The system's response to the presence of a car is defined by a pattern that ensures if a car is present, its light will be turned green in response, but this response may involve an intermediate state where the system remembers that a car was present but its light was not yet green. This pattern ensures that the system will periodically return to a state where it is ready to respond to new car arrivals.
