The TrafficE2 system controls traffic flow based on input from the environment. The environment provides signals indicating the presence of cars A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for cars A and B (`greena` and `greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, car A will not be present in the next time step unless the emergency signal remains active or the traffic light for car A is green. Similarly, when the emergency signal is active, car B will not be present in the next time step unless the emergency signal remains active, the traffic light for car B is green, or car B is not currently present. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active, car A will move (i.e., not be present) in the next time step unless the emergency signal remains active, car A is not currently present, or the traffic light for car A is not green. Car B is also expected to move when the emergency signal is active, unless car B is not currently present or the traffic light for car B is not green.

The system guarantees that when car A is present, the traffic light for car A will eventually turn green, and when car B is present, the traffic light for car B will eventually turn green. This is achieved through a response pattern where the system initially waits for a request (car presence). If a request is made and not immediately granted, the system transitions to a pending state. The system remains in this pending state until the request is granted, at which point it returns to the initial waiting state. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in the waiting state, indicating that requests are eventually granted. 

Initially, the system is in the waiting state. The system's safety requirements ensure that it will always transition between the waiting and pending states correctly, based on the presence of cars and the traffic lights.
