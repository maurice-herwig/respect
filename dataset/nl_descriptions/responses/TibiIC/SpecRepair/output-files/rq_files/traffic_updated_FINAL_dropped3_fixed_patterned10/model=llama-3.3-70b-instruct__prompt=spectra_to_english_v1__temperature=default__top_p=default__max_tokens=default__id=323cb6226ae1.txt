The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green for roads A and B (`greena` and `greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the traffic light for road A is red, then there should be no car on road A; when the emergency signal is active, the traffic light for road B should be green if there is no emergency; when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the traffic light for road A will turn red at the next time step; and when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next time step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road A to green; and when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road B to green.

The system's response to cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars; if a car is present on a road and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system moves to a state where it must respond to the car; once the system responds by setting the traffic light to green, it returns to the initial state, allowing it to respond to the presence of cars again; this pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars on the roads.
