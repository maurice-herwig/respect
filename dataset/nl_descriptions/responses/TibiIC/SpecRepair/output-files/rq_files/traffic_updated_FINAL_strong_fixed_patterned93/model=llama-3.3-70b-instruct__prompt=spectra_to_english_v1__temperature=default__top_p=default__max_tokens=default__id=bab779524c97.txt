The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal from the environment. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: it will not have an emergency situation infinitely often, and when a car is present and its corresponding light is red while the other car's light is green, the car will remain present in the next state. Additionally, when a car is present and its corresponding light is green, the car will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for car A and car B will not both be red at the same time. If car A is present, the system will eventually turn its green light on, and if car B is present, the system will eventually turn its green light on. When an emergency situation occurs, both green lights will be turned off.

The system's response to the presence of a car is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually turn the corresponding green light on when the car is present. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car's presence. The system stays in this state as long as the car is not present or its green light is on. If the car is present and its green light is off, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the car's presence. The system stays in this responding state until the car's green light is turned on, at which point it transitions back to the non-responding state. The system will infinitely often be in the non-responding state, ensuring that it will eventually respond to the car's presence.
