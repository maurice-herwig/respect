The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by cara and carb, respectively, as well as an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with greena and greenb indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, it is assumed that the emergency signal is not always active, and it will eventually become inactive. When the emergency signal is active, the environment will not have a car on road A unless the light is green for road A, the emergency signal remains active, or the car was already present. Similarly, when the emergency signal is active, the environment will not have a car on road B unless the light is green for road B or the emergency signal remains active. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active and the light is green for road A, the car on road A will move, unless the emergency signal remains active or the light turns red. The same applies to road B.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the light green for road A. The same applies to road B. Furthermore, when the emergency signal is active, the system will turn both lights red.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. The system's response to the presence of cars is as follows: when a car is present on a road, the system will turn the corresponding light green, unless the car is no longer present. The system will continue to turn the light green as long as the car is present, and it will turn the light red when the car is no longer present. This behavior repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the traffic lights respond to the presence of cars on both roads.
