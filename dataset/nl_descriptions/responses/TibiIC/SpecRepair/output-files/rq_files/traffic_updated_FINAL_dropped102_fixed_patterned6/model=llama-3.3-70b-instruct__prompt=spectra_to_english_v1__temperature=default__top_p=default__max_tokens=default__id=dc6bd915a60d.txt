The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an `emergency` flag. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: when an emergency occurs, the next state will have a car on road A if the current state does not already have one. Similarly, when an emergency occurs, the next state will either have a car on road B, or the emergency will still be active, or the light for road B will be green, or there will not be a car on road B. Additionally, when an emergency is present, if there is a car on road A, it will move (i.e., its presence will be false in the next state) unless the light for road A is not green. The same logic applies to road B: if there is an emergency and a car on road B, the car will move unless the emergency is still present, or there is no car on road B, or the light for road B is not green.

The system guarantees several behaviors. First, if there is no emergency, the light for road B will not be green. Second, if a car is present on road A and the light for road B is not green, the system will eventually respond by making the light for road A green. Third, if a car is present on road B and the light for road B is green, the system will maintain this state. Finally, if there is no emergency, both lights will not be red at the same time.

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of a car. If a car is present on a road and the corresponding light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system will return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system will always eventually respond to the presence of a car on either road.
