The TrafficE2 system interacts with an environment that controls three boolean inputs: `car`, indicating the presence of a car, `emergency`, and `police`. The system controls one boolean output: `green`, representing a traffic light.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the car is not present, it will either remain absent or become present only when the traffic light is green. Additionally, when the police are present, the car will either be absent or will not be present in the next state, unless the traffic light is not green.

The system guarantees that the traffic light will often be green when a car is present. This is achieved through a pattern of behavior where the system responds to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. 

Initially, the system is in a state where it is prepared to respond to the car's presence. The system then enters a cycle of states, where it remains in the initial state if there is no car or if the car is present and the light is green. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a next state, where it remains until the light turns green, at which point it returns to the initial state. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in the initial state, allowing it to respond to the car's presence.
