The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and whether there is an emergency. The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green for either road A (`greena`) or road B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road A is red, the next state will either have a car on road A, still be in an emergency, or have the traffic light for road A turn green. Similarly, for road B, when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road B is red, the next state will either have a car on road B, still be in an emergency, have the traffic light for road B turn green, or not have a car on road B. Additionally, when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road A is green, the next state will either still be in an emergency or have the traffic light for road A turn red. The traffic light for road B will be red when there is an emergency.

The system guarantees the following behaviors: the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When there is a car on road A, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road A to green. Similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road B to green. However, during an emergency, the system will keep the traffic lights off (i.e., red), as indicated by the guarantee that the emergency condition implies the traffic lights are not green.

The system's response to cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present on a road and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system moves to a state where it must respond by setting the traffic light to green. Once the traffic light is set to green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system eventually responds to the presence of cars on both roads A and B by setting the corresponding traffic lights to green.
