The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal from the environment. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that the emergency signal is not always present, and it will eventually become false. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red, it will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present, its light is red, and car A is not present, it will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when car A is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B: when it is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B: when it is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. If an emergency signal is present, the system will ensure that both lights are red.

Initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars. The system will always be in one of two states: waiting for a car to be present and then responding to it, or waiting for the car's light to turn green after it has been present. The system will infinitely often return to the waiting state, ensuring that it can respond to cars. 

In each state, the system's behavior is determined by the presence of cars and the current state of the lights. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system will move to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. If a car's light is green, the system will move to a state where it is waiting for the car to move. If a car is not present or its light is green, the system will remain in the waiting state.
