The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars A and B, as well as the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for cars A and B.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: it will not have an emergency situation infinitely often, and when a car is present and its corresponding light is red while the other car's light is green, the car will remain present in the next state. Additionally, when a car is present and its corresponding light is green, the car will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for cars A and B will not both be red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its green light on, and when car B is present, the system will eventually turn its green light on. If an emergency situation occurs, both green lights will be turned off.

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually respond to the presence of a car by turning its corresponding green light on. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the presence of a car. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the presence of a car. The system will remain in this state as long as the car is not present or its green light is on. If the car is present and its green light is off, the system will transition to a state where it is responding to the presence of the car. The system will remain in this state until the car's green light is turned on, at which point it will transition back to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it will eventually respond to the presence of a car.
