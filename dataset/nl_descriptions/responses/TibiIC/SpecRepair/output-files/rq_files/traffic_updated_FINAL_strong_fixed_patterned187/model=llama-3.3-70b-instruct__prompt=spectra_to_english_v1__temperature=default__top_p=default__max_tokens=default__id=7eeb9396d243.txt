The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to the presence of these cars and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and emergency conditions. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally expected that there will be no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red and there is no emergency, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red, car B will remain present in the next state. Conversely, when car A is present and its light is green, car A will move and not be present in the next state, and the same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same guarantee applies to car B. In the event of an emergency, the system ensures that both lights are red.

The system's response to the presence of cars is defined by a pattern that ensures the system responds to the stimulus (car presence) by providing a permit (green light) in a way that satisfies specific safety and liveness conditions. Initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the stimulus. The system remains in this state as long as the stimulus is not present or is present and the permit is given. If the stimulus is present and the permit is not given, the system moves to a state where it must provide the permit to return to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it periodically responds to the stimulus.
