The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system, in turn, controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it will not have police interventions frequently, and when a car is present, the traffic light will be green or the car will not be moving. Additionally, when a car is moving, it will either be during a police intervention, or the car will stop moving at the next instant, or the traffic light will not be green.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is defined by a pattern where the system starts in an initial state. In this state, if a car is present, the traffic light will be green. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system moves to a next state. In this next state, if the traffic light becomes green, the system returns to the initial state. The system will infinitely often return to its initial state.

The system's behavior is constrained by the environment's assumptions. When a police intervention occurs, a car will either not be moving at the next instant or the traffic light will not be green. The traffic light's green state is directly related to the presence of a car, and the system ensures that the traffic light turns green in response to a car's presence, following the specified pattern.
