The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. When the emergency signal is not active, if the light for road A is red, the environment will ensure that a car is present on road A at the next time step. Similarly, when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road B is red, the environment will ensure that a car is present on road B at the next time step, unless the light for road B is already green or there is no car on road B. Additionally, when the emergency signal is not active, if the light for road A is green, the environment will ensure that there is no car on road A at the next time step, unless the light for road A turns red. The environment also behaves similarly for road B when the emergency signal is active.

The system must satisfy certain guarantees. The traffic lights must never both be red, unless the emergency signal is active. When a car is present on road A and the light for road B is red, the system must eventually turn the light for road A green. When a car is present on road B and the light for road B is green, the system must keep the light for road B green. During an emergency, the system must turn both lights red.

The system's behavior is also defined by a pattern that describes how the system responds to stimuli. Initially, the system is in a state where it is waiting for a stimulus. The system remains in this state as long as the stimulus is not present or the corresponding response is present. If the stimulus is present and the response is not, the system transitions to a new state. In this new state, the system will remain until the response is present, at which point it will transition back to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, waiting for a stimulus.
