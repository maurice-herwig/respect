The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency conditions. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an emergency flag. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, denoted by `greena` and `greenb`, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when an emergency is in effect, it will not start a car on road A unless the light is green or the emergency condition persists into the next state, or the car was already present. Similarly, for road B, the environment will not start a car unless the light is green, the emergency condition persists, the car was not present, or the car's presence persists into the next state. Additionally, when an emergency is in effect, the environment will ensure that if a car is present on road A, it will either move (by not being present in the next state) or the emergency condition will persist, or the light will turn red. A similar assumption is made for road B, where if a car is present, it will either move or the light will turn red.

The system guarantees that whenever a car is present on road A, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road A green, and similarly for road B. This response is governed by a pattern that ensures the system initially starts in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of a car. The system remains in this state as long as no car is present or a car is present and the corresponding light is green. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must respond by turning the light green. Once the light is green, the system can return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. The system ensures that it infinitely often returns to this initial ready state.
