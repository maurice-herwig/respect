The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and emergency conditions. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), the presence of cars on road B (`carb`), and whether an emergency is occurring (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The system must always ensure that at least one of the roads has a red light, meaning that either road A's light is not green or road B's light is not green. When a car is present on road A, the system must eventually respond by turning road A's light green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system must eventually respond by turning road B's light green.

However, during an emergency, the system must keep both lights red, as indicated by the requirement that there is no emergency when either light is green. 

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: when an emergency occurs and road A's light is red, the car on road A will not move until either the emergency ends, road A's light turns green, or the car on road A moves away. Similarly, when an emergency occurs and road B's light is red, the car on road B will not move until either the emergency ends, road B's light turns green, or there is no car on road B. Additionally, during an emergency, if road A's light is green, the car on road A will move away, and road B's light will remain red.

The system's behavior is also influenced by a pattern that defines how it responds to the presence of cars on each road. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. The system remains in this state as long as there are no cars present or if a car is present and the corresponding light is green. If a car is present and the corresponding light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the light green. Once the light is green, the system returns to its initial state. The system will infinitely often be in its initial state, ready to respond to the presence of cars.
