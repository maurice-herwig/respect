The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system, in turn, controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways: it should not have police interventions frequently, and when a car is present, the traffic light should be green or the car should not be moving. Additionally, when the police are present or the traffic light is not green, the car should either not be moving or should stop at the next opportunity.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by turning the traffic light green. This response is governed by a pattern that ensures the system initially starts in a state where it is ready to respond to the car's presence. The system then continuously checks for the car's presence and adjusts its state accordingly. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it waits for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. The system ensures that it frequently returns to this initial state, allowing it to consistently respond to cars.

The traffic light starts in a state where it is ready to turn green when a car is present. The system maintains safety by ensuring that the traffic light and the car's presence are coordinated correctly, and it ensures liveness by frequently returning to its initial state, allowing it to respond to cars. The system updates its state based on the presence of the car and the police, and the current state of the traffic light.
