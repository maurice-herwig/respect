The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. Additionally, when car A is present and the green light for car A is red while the green light for car B is on, car A will remain present in the next time step. Similarly, when car B is present and the green light for car B is red while the green light for car A is on, car B will remain present in the next time step. Furthermore, when car A is present and its green light is on, car A will move and no longer be present in the next time step. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the green lights for car A and car B are never both off at the same time. If car A is present, the system will eventually turn on the green light for car A. Similarly, if car B is present, the system will eventually turn on the green light for car B. When an emergency signal is present, the system will turn off both green lights. 

The system's response to the presence of cars is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually respond to a car's presence by turning on its corresponding green light. This pattern is initially in a state where the system is not waiting for a response, and it remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its green light is already on. If a car is present and its green light is off, the system enters a state where it is waiting for a response, and it will remain in this state until the green light is turned on. The system will always eventually return to the initial state.
