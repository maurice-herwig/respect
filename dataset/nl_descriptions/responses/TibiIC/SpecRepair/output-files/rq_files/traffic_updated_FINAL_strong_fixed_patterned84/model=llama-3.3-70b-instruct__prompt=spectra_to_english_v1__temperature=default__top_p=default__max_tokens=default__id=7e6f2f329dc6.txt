The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal from the environment. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red, the environment will keep car A present in the next time step. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red, the environment will keep car B present in the next time step. However, when car A is present and its light is green, the environment will ensure that car A is no longer present in the next time step. For car B, when it is present, its light is green, and car A is also present, the environment will ensure that car B is no longer present in the next time step.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. Similarly, when car B is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

Initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars. The system remains in this state as long as no car is present or a car is present and its corresponding light is green. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the light green. Once the light is turned green, the system returns to its initial state. This pattern of responding to the presence of cars repeats infinitely often.
