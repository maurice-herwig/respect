The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (cara) and road B (carb), as well as the occurrence of emergencies. The system controls the traffic lights for road A (greena) and road B (greenb).

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when an emergency occurs, the environment ensures that if a car is on road A, it will either remain idle or the traffic light for road A will turn green, or the emergency will persist. Similar assumptions are made for road B. The environment also ensures that when an emergency occurs and the traffic light for a road is green, the car on that road will move, either immediately or at the next opportunity, or the emergency will persist.

The system guarantees that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road B green. During an emergency, the system ensures that if the emergency is not ongoing, then either the traffic light for road A or the traffic light for road B (or both) will be red.

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present on a road and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system will return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system will always eventually respond to the presence of cars on the roads by turning the corresponding traffic lights green.
