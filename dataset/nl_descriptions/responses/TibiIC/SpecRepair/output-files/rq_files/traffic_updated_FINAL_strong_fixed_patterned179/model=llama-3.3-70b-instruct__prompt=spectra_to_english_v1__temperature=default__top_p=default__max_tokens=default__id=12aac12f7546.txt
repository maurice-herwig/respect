The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to the presence of these cars and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and emergency conditions. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally expected that there will be no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red, it is assumed that car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present, its light is red, and there is no emergency, car B is assumed to remain present in the next state. Conversely, when car A or car B is present and its respective light is green, it is assumed that the car will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system ensures that both lights turn red.

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B is defined by a pattern that ensures the system responds to the stimulus (car presence) by providing a permit (green light) in a way that the absence of the stimulus or the presence of the permit at the same time does not lead to an inconsistent state. This pattern also ensures that once the system is in a state where it needs to respond to the stimulus, it will do so in a way that leads back to a stable state where the stimulus is either absent or the permit is present. The system is designed to infinitely often be in a state where it is ready to respond to new stimuli.
