The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and emergency situations. The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when an emergency occurs, the environment is assumed to behave in specific ways: if road A is red (i.e., `greena` is false), the next state will either have a car on road A, still be in an emergency, or have the traffic light for road A turn green; similarly, if road B is red, the next state will either have a car on road B, still be in an emergency, or have the traffic light for road B turn green. The environment is also assumed to have cars move when their respective traffic lights are green during an emergency.

The system guarantees several behaviors. First, it ensures that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. Second, when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green; similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road B green. Third, during an emergency, the system will ensure that both traffic lights are red.

The system's behavior is also governed by an initial condition: initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system then enters a loop where it responds to the presence of cars on the roads by turning the corresponding traffic lights green, and it does so in a way that ensures the traffic lights are never both red at the same time. This behavior is repeated indefinitely, ensuring that the system responds to the presence of cars on the roads in a timely and safe manner.
