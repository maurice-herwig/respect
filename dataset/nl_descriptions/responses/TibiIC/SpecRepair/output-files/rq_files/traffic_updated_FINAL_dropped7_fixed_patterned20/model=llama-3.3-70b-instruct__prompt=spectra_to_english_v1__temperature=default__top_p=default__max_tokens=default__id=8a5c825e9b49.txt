The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (cara and carb) and whether there is an emergency. The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green (greena and greenb) for roads A and B.

The environment is assumed to behave as follows: when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road A is red, the next state will either have a car on road A, the emergency will continue, or the traffic light for road A will be green. Similarly, for road B, when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road B is red, the next state will either have a car on road B, the emergency will continue, the traffic light for road B will be green, or there will be no car on road B. Additionally, when there is an emergency, if the traffic light for road A is green, the next state will either have no car on road A or the traffic light for road A will be red. The traffic light for road B will be red when there is an emergency.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When there is a car on road A, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road A to green. Similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually set the traffic light for road B to green. However, when there is an emergency, the system will keep the traffic lights in a safe state, effectively keeping them red, as the guarantee that the emergency is false is always true, indicating the system's response to an emergency is to prioritize safety.

The system's response to cars on the roads follows a pattern where the presence of a car on a road leads to the traffic light for that road being set to green. This pattern involves the system tracking its state and responding accordingly to ensure that when a car is present, the corresponding traffic light will be set to green, and once the light is green, it will stay green until the car is gone or the system resets its state. The system always returns to a safe state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars on the roads.
