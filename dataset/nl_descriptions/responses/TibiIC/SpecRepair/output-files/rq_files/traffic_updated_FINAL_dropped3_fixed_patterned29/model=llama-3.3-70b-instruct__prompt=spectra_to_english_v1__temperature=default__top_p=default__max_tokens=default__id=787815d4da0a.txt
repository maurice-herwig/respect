The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road A is red, then there must be no car on road A; when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road B is red, then the emergency signal must still be active; when the emergency signal is not active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the light for road A will turn red at the next step; and when the emergency signal is not active or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road A green; and when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road B green.

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of a car. If a car is present and the corresponding light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system responds to the presence of cars on the roads in a timely manner.
