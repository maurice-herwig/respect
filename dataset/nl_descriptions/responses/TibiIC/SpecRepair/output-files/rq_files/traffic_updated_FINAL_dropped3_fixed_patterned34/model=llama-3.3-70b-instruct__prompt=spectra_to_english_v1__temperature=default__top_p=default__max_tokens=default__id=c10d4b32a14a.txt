The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (cara and carb) and an emergency status. The system controls the traffic lights, with greena and greenb indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is true, if the light for road A is red, then there is no car on road A; when the emergency status is true, the light for road B is either green or the emergency status remains true; when the emergency status is true or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move (i.e., its presence will change) or the light for road A will turn red at the next time step; similarly, when the emergency status is true or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next time step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road A green; similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road B green.

The system's response to cars on the roads is governed by a pattern that ensures the system will respond to a car's presence by turning the corresponding light green. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to a car's presence. The system remains in this state as long as there is no car present or the light is already green. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. The system will infinitely often be in its initial state, ensuring that it will repeatedly respond to cars on the roads.
