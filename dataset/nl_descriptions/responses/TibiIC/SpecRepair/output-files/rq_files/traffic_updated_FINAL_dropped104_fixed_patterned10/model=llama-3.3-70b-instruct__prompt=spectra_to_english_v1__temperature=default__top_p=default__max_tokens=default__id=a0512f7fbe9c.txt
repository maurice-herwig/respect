The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and an emergency status. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by cara and carb, respectively, as well as the emergency status. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, denoted by greena and greenb, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is true, if a car is present on road A, it will either remain present, the emergency status will remain true, or the traffic light for road A will be green. Similarly, for road B, if a car is present, it will either remain present, the emergency status will remain true, the traffic light for road B will be green, or there will be no car on road B. Additionally, when the emergency status is true, if there is a car on road A, the car will either not be present in the next state, the emergency status will remain true, or the traffic light for road A will not be green. A similar assumption is made for road B: if there is a car on road B, it will either not be present in the next state or the traffic light for road B will not be green.

The system guarantees that whenever a car is present on road A, the traffic light for road A will eventually be green, and similarly for road B. This guarantee is fulfilled by the system responding to the presence of cars on each road in a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of a car. If a car is present and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must respond by turning the traffic light green. Once the traffic light is green, the system returns to its initial state, allowing it to respond to the next car presence. The system always eventually returns to this initial state, ensuring that it can continuously respond to car presences on both roads.
