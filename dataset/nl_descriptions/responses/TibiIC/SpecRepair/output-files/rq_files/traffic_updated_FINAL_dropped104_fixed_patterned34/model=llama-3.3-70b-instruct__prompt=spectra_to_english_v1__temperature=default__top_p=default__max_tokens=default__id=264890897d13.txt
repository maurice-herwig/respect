The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, denoted by `greena` and `greenb`, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if a car is present on road A, it will either remain present, the emergency signal will remain active, or the traffic light for road A will be green. Similarly, for road B, if a car is present, it will either remain present, the emergency signal will remain active, the traffic light for road B will be green, or there will be no car on road B. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active, if there is a car on road A, it will either not be present in the next state, the emergency signal will remain active, or the traffic light for road A will not be green. A similar assumption is made for road B.

The system guarantees that if a car is present on road A, the traffic light for road A will eventually be green, and similarly for road B. This guarantee is met by the system responding to the presence of cars on each road in a specific pattern. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to a car. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the car. The system remains in this state until the traffic light is green, at which point it transitions back to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it periodically checks for the presence of cars and responds accordingly.
