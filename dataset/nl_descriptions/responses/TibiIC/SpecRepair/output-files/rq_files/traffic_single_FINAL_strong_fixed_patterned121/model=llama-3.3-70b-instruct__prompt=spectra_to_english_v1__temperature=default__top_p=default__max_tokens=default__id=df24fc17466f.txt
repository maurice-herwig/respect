The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, specifically the green signal.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, it is assumed that police interventions do not occur infinitely often. Additionally, when the traffic light is not green and there is no emergency, if a car is present, it will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when the traffic light is green and a car is present, the car will move and not be present in the next state.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic light will turn green infinitely often when a car is present. Additionally, the system ensures that there are infinitely many times when no car is present.

The system starts in an initial state where it is ready to respond to the presence of a car. The system's behavior can be described by a set of rules that determine its next state based on the current state and the presence of a car. The system will periodically return to its initial state, ensuring that it can respond to the presence of a car. 

In more detail, when the system is in its initial state, it will remain in that state if a car is not present or if a car is present and the traffic light is green. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system will transition to a different state. From this different state, the system will return to its initial state if the traffic light turns green, and it will remain in this state if the traffic light does not turn green. The system will infinitely often be in its initial state, allowing it to respond to the presence of a car.
