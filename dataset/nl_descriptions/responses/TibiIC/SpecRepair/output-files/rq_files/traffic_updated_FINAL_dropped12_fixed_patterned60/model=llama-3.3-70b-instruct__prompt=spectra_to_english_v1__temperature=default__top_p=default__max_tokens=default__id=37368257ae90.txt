The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), the presence of cars on road B (`carb`), and whether there is an emergency (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies frequently, meaning that it is generally not in an emergency state. Additionally, when the traffic light for road A is red, the environment ensures that there is no car on road A or an emergency is present. If there is an emergency, the environment maintains the emergency state until the traffic light for road B is green. When the traffic light for road A is green, the environment ensures that any car on road A moves. Similarly, when the traffic light for road B is green and there is no emergency, the environment ensures that any car on road B moves.

The system guarantees that when there is no emergency, the traffic light for road B is not green if the emergency state is false. The system also ensures that if there is a car on road A, it will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. If there is a car on road B, the system will eventually turn either the traffic light for road A or road B green. During an emergency, the system ensures that both traffic lights are red. 

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, it is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the traffic light green. Once the traffic light is green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars on the roads.
