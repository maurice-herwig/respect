The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars A and B, as well as the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for cars A and B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally expected that there will be no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when car A's light is green and car A is present, it will move and no longer be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for cars A and B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, both lights will turn red. 

The system's behavior can be described as follows: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars A and B. The system remains in this state as long as the presence of a car does not require its light to be turned green. If a car's presence requires its light to be turned green and it is not, the system moves to a state where it must turn the light green. Once the light is turned green, the system returns to its initial state, allowing it to respond to the presence of cars A and B again. This process repeats infinitely often, ensuring that the system responds to the presence of cars A and B in a timely manner.
