The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and emergency conditions. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red, the environment will keep car A present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present, its light is red, and there is no emergency, the environment will keep car B present in the next state. Conversely, when car A or car B is present and its corresponding light is green, the environment will ensure that the car moves and is no longer present in the next state.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B is defined by a pattern that ensures the system eventually responds to the presence of a car by turning its corresponding light green. This pattern starts in an initial state where the system is not waiting for a response. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a waiting state. The system stays in this waiting state until the car's light is turned green, at which point it returns to the initial state. The system always eventually returns to this initial state.
