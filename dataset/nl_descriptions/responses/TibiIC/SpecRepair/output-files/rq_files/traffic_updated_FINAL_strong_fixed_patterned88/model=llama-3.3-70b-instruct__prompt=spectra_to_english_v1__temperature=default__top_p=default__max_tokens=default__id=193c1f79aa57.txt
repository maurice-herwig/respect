The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain idle until its light turns green. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain idle until its light turns green. Furthermore, when car A's light is green and car A is present, car A will move and its presence will be removed. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B will not both be red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

The system's behavior is also guided by a pattern that ensures responsiveness to stimuli. Initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of cars. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is green when the car is present. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the car's light green to return to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it periodically returns to a state where it can respond to the presence of cars.
