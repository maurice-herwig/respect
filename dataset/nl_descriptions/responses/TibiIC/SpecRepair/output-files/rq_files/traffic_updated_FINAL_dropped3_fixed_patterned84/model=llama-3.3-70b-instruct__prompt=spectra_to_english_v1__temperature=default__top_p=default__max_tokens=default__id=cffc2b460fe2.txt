The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting `greena` and `greenb` to indicate which road has the right of way.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is active, if road A's light is red, then there is no car on road A; similarly, when the emergency status is active, if road B's light is red, then the emergency status remains active or road B's light turns green. Additionally, when the emergency status is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or its presence signal will be reset at the next time step, unless road A's light is red. A similar assumption applies to road B: when the emergency status is active or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next time step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the traffic lights are designed so that both roads are never red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually set road A's light to green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually set road B's light to green.

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of a car. If a car is present on a road and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars on the roads.
