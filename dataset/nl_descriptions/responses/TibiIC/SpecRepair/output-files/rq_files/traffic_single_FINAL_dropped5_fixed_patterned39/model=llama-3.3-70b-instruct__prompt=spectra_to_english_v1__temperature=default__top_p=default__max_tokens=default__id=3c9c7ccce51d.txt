The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal. 

The environment is expected to behave in a specific manner: when the traffic light is green, the car will either remain present or arrive in the next time step, or the light will remain green and the car will be absent. Additionally, if the police are present, the car will either be absent or will leave in the next time step, or the traffic light will not be green.

Given these environmental assumptions, the system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is defined by a pattern where the system starts in an initial state. The system then follows specific rules: if a car is present and the light is not green, it moves to a state where it remembers the car's presence; if the car is absent or the light is green when a car is present, it remains in its current state or returns to the initial state if the light becomes green after a car has been waiting.

The system ensures that it will infinitely often return to its initial state, indicating that it periodically responds to the car's presence by turning the traffic light green. The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions, while the system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, specifically the green signal.
