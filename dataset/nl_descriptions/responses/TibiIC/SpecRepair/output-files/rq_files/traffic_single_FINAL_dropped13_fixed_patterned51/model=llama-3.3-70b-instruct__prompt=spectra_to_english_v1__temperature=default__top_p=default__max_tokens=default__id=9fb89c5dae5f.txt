The TrafficE2 system controls a traffic light, interacting with its environment. The environment controls three inputs: `car`, indicating whether a car is present, `emergency`, and `police`, which are boolean signals. The system controls one output: `green`, indicating whether the traffic light is green.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: the `police` signal will be false infinitely often, and when the traffic light is green, the car will either remain present or the light will stay green at the next time step, or the car will not be present. Additionally, when the traffic light is about to turn green, the car will move, meaning it will not be present at the next time step.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by turning the traffic light green. This means that whenever a car is present, the system will eventually turn the traffic light green. The system starts in an initial state where it is not responding to the car's presence. The system's behavior can be described by a state machine that tracks whether it is currently responding to the car's presence. 

In this state machine, the system remains in the initial state as long as there is no car present or the traffic light is green when a car is present. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system moves to a different state. In this different state, the system will turn the traffic light green, and once it does, it will return to the initial state. If the traffic light is not green in this state, the system will stay in this state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it periodically checks for the presence of a car and responds accordingly.
