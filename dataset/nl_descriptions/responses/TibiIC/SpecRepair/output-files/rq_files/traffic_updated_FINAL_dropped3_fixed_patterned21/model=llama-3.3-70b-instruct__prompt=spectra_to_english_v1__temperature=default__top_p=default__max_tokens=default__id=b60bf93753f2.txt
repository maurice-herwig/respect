The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), the presence of cars on road B (`carb`), and an emergency signal (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The system must ensure that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system must eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system must eventually turn the traffic light for road B green.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the traffic light for road A is red, then there is no car on road A; when the emergency signal is active, the traffic light for road B is green; when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the traffic light for road A will turn red at the next step; and when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next step.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is waiting for a car to arrive on either road. The system then responds to the presence of cars on each road by turning the corresponding traffic light green. If a car is present on a road and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, waiting for the next car to arrive. The system must infinitely often return to this initial state.
