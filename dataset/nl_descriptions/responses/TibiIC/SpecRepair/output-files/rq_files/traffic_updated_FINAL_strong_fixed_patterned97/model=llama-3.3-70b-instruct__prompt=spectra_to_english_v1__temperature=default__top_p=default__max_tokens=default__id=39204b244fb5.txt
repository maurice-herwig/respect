The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and the light for car A is red while the light for car B is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and the light for car B is red while the light for car A is green, car B will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when car A is present and its light is green, car A will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for car A green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system will ensure that both lights are red. 

In the system's response to the presence of cars, it can be in one of two states: waiting for a car to be served or waiting for the car to be served to actually have its light turned green. Initially, the system is in the waiting state. The system remains in the waiting state if a car is not present or if a car is present and its light is already green. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to the state where it is waiting for the car's light to be turned green. The system returns to the waiting state when the car's light is turned green. The system will infinitely often be in the waiting state.
