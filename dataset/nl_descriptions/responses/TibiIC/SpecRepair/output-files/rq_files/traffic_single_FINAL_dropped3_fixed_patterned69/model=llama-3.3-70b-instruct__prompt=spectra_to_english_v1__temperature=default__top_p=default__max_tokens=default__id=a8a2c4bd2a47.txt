The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system-controlled output is the green traffic light signal.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, it is assumed that the police will not be present indefinitely, and there will be times when the police are not present. Additionally, when the police are about to arrive, the car will either be present or the traffic light will be green, or the car will not be present. The environment is also assumed to ensure that if a car is present, it will either not be present in the next moment or the traffic light will not be green.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic light will turn green often, and whenever a car is present, the system will respond by eventually turning the traffic light green. The system also guarantees that there will be times when no car is present.

Initially, the system starts in a state where it is not responding to the presence of a car. The system's behavior can be described by a set of rules that define its safety and liveness properties. The system will always be in one of two states: either it is not responding to the car's presence, or it is responding. If the system is not responding and a car is present but the traffic light is not green, it will transition to the responding state. If the system is responding and the traffic light is green, it will transition back to the non-responding state. The system will infinitely often be in the non-responding state.
