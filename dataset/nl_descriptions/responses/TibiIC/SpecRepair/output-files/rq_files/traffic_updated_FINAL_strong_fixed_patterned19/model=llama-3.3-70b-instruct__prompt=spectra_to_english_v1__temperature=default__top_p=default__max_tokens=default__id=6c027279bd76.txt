The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal from the environment. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it will not have an emergency signal active often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when car A is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees certain behaviors: the lights for car A and car B will never both be red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually respond by turning its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency signal is active, both lights will be red. 

The system's response to the presence of a car is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually turn the corresponding light green when the car is present. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is already green. If the car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the car. In this state, the system will turn the car's light green, and then transition back to the initial state. The system will always eventually return to the initial state, ensuring that it will respond to the car's presence again in the future.
