The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: it will not have an emergency situation often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. When car A is present and its light is red, it will remain present in the next time step. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red, and there is no emergency, it will remain present in the next time step. On the other hand, when car A is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next time step. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees the following behavior: it will never have both green lights off at the same time, ensuring that at least one of the cars can proceed. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its green light on. The same applies to car B. If an emergency situation occurs, the system will turn off both green lights. 

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will respond to the presence of a car by turning its green light on. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the presence of a car. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the presence of a car. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its green light is on. If the car is present and its green light is off, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the presence of the car. The system remains in this state until the car's green light is turned on, at which point it transitions back to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it will repeatedly respond to the presence of cars.
