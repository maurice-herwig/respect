The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on each road and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), the presence of cars on road B (`carb`), and the emergency signal (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the traffic light for road A is red, then there must be no car on road A; when the emergency signal is active, if the traffic light for road B is red, then the emergency signal must remain active; when the emergency signal is not active or there is no car on road A, or the car on road A will move away at the next step, or the traffic light for road A is red, then the car on road A must move away; similarly, when the emergency signal is not active or there is no car on road B, or the car on road B will move away at the next step, then the car on road B must move away.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green; when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road B green.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is waiting for a car to arrive. The system then responds to the arrival of cars on roads A and B by turning the corresponding traffic lights green. The system ensures that once a car arrives, the corresponding traffic light will be turned green, and it will remain green until the car moves away. If a car is present and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system will turn the traffic light green at some point in the future. The traffic lights will always be turned green in response to the presence of cars, as long as the environment behaves according to the assumptions.
