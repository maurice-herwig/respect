The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals `cara` and `carb`, indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B, respectively, as well as an `emergency` signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road A is not green, then there must be no car on road A. Similarly, when the emergency signal is active, if the light for road B is not green, then the emergency signal must remain active. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move (i.e., `cara` will become false) or the light for road A will not be green. A similar assumption is made for road B: when the emergency signal is active or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move (i.e., `carb` will become false).

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. Furthermore, the system responds to the presence of cars on the roads: if a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the light for road A green, and similarly for road B. This response is governed by a pattern that ensures the system will periodically return to a state where it is ready to respond to new cars.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to new cars. The system then enters a cycle where it responds to the presence of cars by turning the corresponding light green, and then returns to the ready state. This cycle repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system always responds to cars on the roads.
