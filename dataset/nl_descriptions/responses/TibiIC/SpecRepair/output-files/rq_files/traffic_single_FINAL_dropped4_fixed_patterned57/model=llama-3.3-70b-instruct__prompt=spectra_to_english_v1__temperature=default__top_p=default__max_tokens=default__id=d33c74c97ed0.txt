The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal. 

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it is expected that there will be periods where the police are not present, and this condition will occur infinitely often. Additionally, when the traffic light is not green, the car will either not be present, be present but not moving, or an emergency will be in progress, and this condition will hold true at all times. The environment is also assumed to ensure that if a car is present, it will either continue to be present or move when the traffic light is green.

The system guarantees that it will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is governed by a pattern that defines the system's behavior in terms of its response to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the car. At all times, the system will be in one of two states: a ready state or a waiting state. 

When in the ready state, if a car is not present or is present and the traffic light is green, the system will remain in the ready state. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system will transition to the waiting state. When in the waiting state, if the traffic light becomes green, the system will return to the ready state; otherwise, it will remain in the waiting state. The system will infinitely often be in the ready state, ensuring that it periodically returns to a state where it can respond to the car's presence. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, indicated by the green signal.
