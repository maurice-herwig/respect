The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A (`cara`), car B (`carb`), and the emergency signal (`emergency`). The system controls the green lights for car A (`greena`) and car B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave as follows: when the emergency signal is active, car A will not be present in the next state unless the emergency signal is still active or the green light for car A is on, or car A is already present. Similarly, when the emergency signal is active, car B will not be present in the next state unless the emergency signal is still active, the green light for car B is on, or car B is not present. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active, if car A is present, it will move (i.e., not be present in the next state) unless the emergency signal is still active or the green light for car A is off. The same applies to car B: when the emergency signal is active, if car B is present, it will move unless the green light for car B is off or car B is not present.

The system guarantees that when car A is present, the green light for car A will eventually be turned on, and when car B is present, the green light for car B will eventually be turned on. This guarantee is met by the system responding to the presence of each car in a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the car's presence. If a car is present and its green light is not on, the system transitions to a state where it must turn on the green light. Once the green light is on, the system returns to its initial state, allowing it to respond to the car's presence again. The system always eventually returns to this initial state, ensuring that it can continue to respond to the cars' presence.
