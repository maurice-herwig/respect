The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways: it should not have police interventions frequently, and when a car is present, it should either be moving when the light is green or idle when the light is red. Additionally, when the light is green, the car should move, and when the light is not green, the car should not move.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by turning the light green. This response is characterized by a pattern where the system initially waits for a car to arrive. When a car is present and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it waits for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, indicating that it has responded to the car's presence. The system ensures that it will repeatedly return to its initial state, indicating frequent responses to the presence of cars.

The system's initial state is one where it is waiting for a car to arrive, and it remains in this state as long as there is no car present or the light is already green when a car arrives. The system's safety requirements ensure that it will only transition between its states in response to specific conditions, such as the presence or absence of a car and the state of the light. The system's liveness requirement ensures that it will infinitely often return to its initial state, indicating that it will frequently respond to the presence of cars. 

Environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police interventions. The system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, indicated by the green signal.
