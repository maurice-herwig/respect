The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green for roads A and B (`greena` and `greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is active, if the light for road A is red, then there should be no car on road A; similarly, if the emergency status is active, then either the emergency status remains active or the light for road B is green. Additionally, when the emergency status is active or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move (i.e., its presence signal will become false) or the light for road A will turn red; a similar assumption is made for road B, where the car will move when the emergency status is active or there is no car on road B.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually set the light for road A to green; similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually set the light for road B to green. This responsiveness is defined by a pattern where the system initially waits for a car to be present, then responds by setting the corresponding light to green when the car is present, and continues to do so as long as the car remains present.

Initially, the system is in a waiting state. The system's safety requirements ensure that it will always respond correctly to the presence of cars on roads A and B, setting the corresponding lights to green when cars are present and turning them red when cars are absent. The system also ensures that it will infinitely often be in the waiting state, ready to respond to new cars arriving at the intersection.
