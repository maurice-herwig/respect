The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting `greena` and `greenb` to indicate when roads A and B have the right of way.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when an emergency is declared, if the light for road A is red, then there is no car on road A; when an emergency is declared, the light for road B is either green or the emergency status remains; when an emergency is declared or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the light will turn red at the next step; and when an emergency is declared or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when a car is present on road A, the system will eventually set the light for road A to green; and when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually set the light for road B to green.

The system's response to the presence of cars on roads A and B follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of a car; if a car is present and the corresponding light is not green, the system moves to a state where it must respond; once the system responds by setting the light to green, it returns to the initial state, allowing it to respond to the next car; this pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars on both roads.
