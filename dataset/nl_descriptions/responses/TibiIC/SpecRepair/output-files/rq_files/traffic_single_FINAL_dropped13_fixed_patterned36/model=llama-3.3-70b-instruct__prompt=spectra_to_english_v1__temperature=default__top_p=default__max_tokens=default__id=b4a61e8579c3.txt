The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency conditions, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways: it is expected that the police will not be present indefinitely, and there will be times when the police are not present. Additionally, the environment is assumed to ensure that a car is either idle or the traffic light is green when the car is present and about to move. Furthermore, in the absence of an emergency, if the car is moving, it will stop when the traffic light turns green.

The system guarantees that it will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green when a car is present. This response is defined by a pattern that describes the system's behavior in terms of its initial state and the transitions between states. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car. The system remains in this state as long as there is no car present or the traffic light is green when a car is present. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the car. The system returns to its initial state when the traffic light turns green, indicating that it has responded to the car. The system will infinitely often return to its initial state, ensuring that it periodically responds to the presence of cars.

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, emergency conditions, and police presence, while the system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, indicated by the green signal.
