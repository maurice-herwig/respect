The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and whether there is an emergency (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting `greena` and `greenb` to indicate which road has the right of way.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. When there is an emergency, if road A's light is red, the environment will either keep `cara` true (indicating a car is present), keep the emergency state, or turn `greena` true (giving road A the right of way) at the next time step. Similarly, when there is an emergency, if road B's light is red, the environment will either keep `carb` true, keep the emergency state, give road B the right of way, or ensure `carb` is false at the next time step. Additionally, during an emergency, if road A has the right of way, the environment will either keep the emergency state, ensure `cara` is false (indicating no car is present) at the next time step, or take away road A's right of way. The environment is also assumed to take away road B's right of way during an emergency.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic lights are designed so that both roads are never given the right of way simultaneously. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually give road A the right of way. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually give road B the right of way. However, during an emergency, the system ensures that the emergency state is maintained, effectively overriding normal operation to prioritize emergency response.

Initially, the system starts in a state where it is prepared to respond to the presence of cars on either road. The system's response to cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: if a car is present on a road and the road does not have the right of way, the system will transition into a state where it is awaiting the right of way to be granted. Once the right of way is granted (and thus the car can proceed), the system will transition back into its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars by granting the right of way, allowing traffic to flow while respecting the constraints imposed by emergencies and the need to avoid simultaneous green lights.
