The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars A and B, as well as emergency conditions. The system controls the green lights for cars A and B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally not in an emergency state. Additionally, when car A is present and the light for car B is green, car A will not move until its own light turns green. Similarly, when car B is present and the light for car A is green, car B will not move until its own light turns green. Furthermore, when a car's light is green, the car will move and its presence will no longer be detected in the next state.

The system guarantees that the lights for cars A and B are never both red at the same time. If car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. When an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. The system also ensures that if a car is present, its corresponding light will be turned green in response, but this response is not immediate and may take some time.

The system's behavior can be understood in terms of a pattern of response to stimuli, where the presence of a car is the stimulus and the corresponding green light is the response. The system starts in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next presence of a car. This pattern of response ensures that the system eventually turns the green light on for a present car, but it does not guarantee a specific timing for this response.
