The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency conditions. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), road B (`carb`), and emergency conditions (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The system must ensure that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system must eventually turn the traffic light for road A to green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system must eventually turn the traffic light for road B to green.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when an emergency occurs, if the traffic light for road A is red, then there must be no car present on road A, or the traffic light for road A must turn green. During an emergency, the environment must ensure that either the emergency condition persists or the traffic light for road B is green. Additionally, when an emergency occurs or there is no car on road A, or if the traffic light for road A is red, then either there will be no car on road A in the next state or there is currently no car on road A. A similar assumption is made for road B: during an emergency or when there is no car on road B, the environment ensures that either the emergency condition persists or there will be no car on road B in the next state.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: when a car is present on a road and the corresponding traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern repeats infinitely, ensuring that the system always responds to the presence of cars on the roads.
