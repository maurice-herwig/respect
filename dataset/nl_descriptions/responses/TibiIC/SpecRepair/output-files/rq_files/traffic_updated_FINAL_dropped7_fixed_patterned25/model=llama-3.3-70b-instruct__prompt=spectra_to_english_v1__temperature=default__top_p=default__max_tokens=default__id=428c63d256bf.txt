The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (cara and carb) and whether there is an emergency. The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green (greena and greenb) for roads A and B.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. When there is an emergency, the environment will not have a car on road A (cara) unless the light is green for road A or the emergency condition persists and the car was already present. Similarly, for road B, when there is an emergency, the environment will not have a car on road B (carb) unless the light is green for road B, the emergency condition persists, or there was no car on road B previously. Additionally, when there is an emergency and the light is green for road A, the environment will ensure that the car on road A moves (cara becomes false) unless the emergency condition persists or the light turns red. The environment is also assumed to keep road B clear (greenb is false) when there is an emergency.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When there is a car on road A, the system will eventually set the light to green for road A. Similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually set the light to green for road B. However, the system does not guarantee that it will respond to the emergency signal by setting the lights to red, as the specification only requires the environment to behave in certain ways during an emergency.

The system starts in a state where it is prepared to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system's response to cars on the roads follows a pattern where it initially waits for a car to arrive. Once a car arrives and the corresponding light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it waits for the light to turn green. When the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system periodically responds to the presence of cars on the roads by setting the corresponding lights to green.
