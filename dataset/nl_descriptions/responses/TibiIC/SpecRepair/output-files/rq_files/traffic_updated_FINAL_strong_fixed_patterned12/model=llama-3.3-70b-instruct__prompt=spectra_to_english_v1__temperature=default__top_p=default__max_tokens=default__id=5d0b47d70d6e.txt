The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next time step. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next time step. Furthermore, when car A is present and its light is green, car A will move and no longer be present in the next time step. The same applies to car B: when car B is present and its light is green, car B will move and no longer be present in the next time step.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are not both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. Similarly, when car B is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually respond to the presence of a car by turning its corresponding light green. This pattern has an initial state where the system is not responding to the car's presence. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is already green. If the car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the car's light to turn green. The system returns to its initial state when the car's light turns green, ensuring that the system will always respond to the car's presence. The system will infinitely often be in its initial state, ensuring that it will always be ready to respond to the presence of cars A and B.
