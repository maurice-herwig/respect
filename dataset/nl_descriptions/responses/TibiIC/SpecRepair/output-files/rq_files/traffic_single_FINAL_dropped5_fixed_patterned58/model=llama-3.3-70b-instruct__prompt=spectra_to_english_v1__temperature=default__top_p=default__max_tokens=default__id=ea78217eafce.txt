The TrafficE2 system controls a traffic light, interacting with its environment, which includes the presence of a car, an emergency, and police. The environment controls the inputs: whether a car is present, whether it's an emergency, and whether the police are present. The system controls the output: whether the traffic light is green.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the traffic light is green, the car will either remain present or the light will stay green or the car will not be present at the next moment. Additionally, if the police are present, or there is no car, or the car will not be present at the next moment, or the traffic light is not green, then the car will either not be present or will not be present at the next moment.

The system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This response is defined by a pattern where the system starts in an initial state, and as long as there is no car or the light is green when a car is present, it remains in this state. If a car is present and the light is not green, it transitions to another state. In this other state, if the light becomes green, it returns to the initial state; otherwise, it stays in this state. The system is required to infinitely often be in its initial state, ensuring that it periodically responds to the car's presence by turning the traffic light green.
