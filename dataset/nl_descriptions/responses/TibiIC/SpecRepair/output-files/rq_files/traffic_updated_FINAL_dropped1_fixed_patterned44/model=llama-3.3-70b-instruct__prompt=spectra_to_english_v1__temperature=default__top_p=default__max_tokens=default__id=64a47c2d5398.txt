The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides input signals indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (cara and carb) and whether there is an emergency (emergency). The system controls the traffic lights, with greena and greenb indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, emergencies do not occur frequently, as there are infinitely many times when there is no emergency. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active, cars on road A will not move unless the light is green or they will move on the next step, and they will not be idle unless the light is red or they were idle on the previous step. Similar assumptions are made for cars on road B. Furthermore, when the emergency signal is active and the light is green for a road, cars on that road will move and will not be present on the next step unless the emergency signal is still active.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When there is a car on road A, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road A green. Similarly, when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually respond by turning the light for road B green. During an emergency, both lights will be red.

Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to cars on the roads. The system's response to cars on the roads follows a specific pattern. When a car is present on a road, the system will eventually turn the corresponding light green. If the light is not green when a car is present, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light is green, the system will return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system responds to cars on the roads in a timely manner.
