The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on road A (`cara`), the presence of cars on road B (`carb`), and whether an emergency is occurring (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights for road A (`greena`) and road B (`greenb`).

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally not in an emergency state. Additionally, when an emergency occurs, the environment ensures that if a car is on road A, it will either remain idle or move next, unless the traffic light for road A is green or the emergency ends. Similar assumptions are made for road B. The environment also ensures that when the traffic light for a road is green during an emergency, the car on that road will move.

The system guarantees that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. The same guarantee is made for road B. Furthermore, when an emergency occurs, the system ensures that both traffic lights will be red.

The system's behavior is also defined by a pattern that describes how it responds to the presence of cars on the roads. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to cars. The system remains in this state as long as there are no cars present or if a car is present and the corresponding traffic light is green. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. This pattern ensures that the system eventually responds to the presence of cars on the roads by turning the corresponding traffic lights green.
