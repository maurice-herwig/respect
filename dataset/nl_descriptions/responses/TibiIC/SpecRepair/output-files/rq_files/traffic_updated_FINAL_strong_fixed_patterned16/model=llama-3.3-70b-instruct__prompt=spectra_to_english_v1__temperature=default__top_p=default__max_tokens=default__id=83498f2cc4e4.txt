The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is always eventually the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next state. When car A is present and its light is green, it will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

In the absence of an emergency, the system responds to the presence of cars by turning their respective lights green. This response is defined by a pattern where the system initially waits for a car to be present. If a car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it waits for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, waiting for the next car presence. This pattern ensures that the system always eventually responds to a car's presence by turning its light green.
