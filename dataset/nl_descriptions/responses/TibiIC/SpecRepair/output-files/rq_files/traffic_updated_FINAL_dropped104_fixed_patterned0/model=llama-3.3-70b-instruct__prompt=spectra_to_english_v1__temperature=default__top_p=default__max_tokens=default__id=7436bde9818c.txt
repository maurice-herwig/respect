The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to cars approaching from these roads and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, indicated by `cara` and `carb`, and the emergency signal, `emergency`. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, indicated by `greena` and `greenb`.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency signal is active, a car will not approach road A unless the light is green or the emergency signal remains active for the next time step, or a car is already approaching. Similarly, for road B, a car will not approach unless the light is green, the emergency signal remains active, or no car is approaching in the next time step. Additionally, when the emergency signal is active, if a car is approaching road A, it will stop approaching in the next time step unless the emergency signal remains active or the light turns red. The same logic applies to road B, with the added condition that if a car is not approaching, it will remain so.

The system guarantees that if a car approaches road A, the light will eventually turn green, and the same applies to road B. The system's behavior is defined by a pattern that ensures the light turns green in response to a car approaching. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to a car approaching. The system remains in this state as long as no car is approaching or the light is green when a car is approaching. If a car approaches and the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the light green. Once the light is green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. The system ensures that it infinitely often returns to its initial state, indicating that it continually responds to cars approaching.
