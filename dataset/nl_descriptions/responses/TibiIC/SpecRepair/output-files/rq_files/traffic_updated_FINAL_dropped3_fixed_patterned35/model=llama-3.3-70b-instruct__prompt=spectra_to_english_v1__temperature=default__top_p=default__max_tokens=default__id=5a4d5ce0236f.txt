The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by the variables `cara` and `carb`, and an emergency status. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is true, if the light for road A is red, then there is no car on road A; when the emergency status is true, the light for road B is green; when the emergency status is true or there is no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the light for road A will turn red at the next time step; and when the emergency status is true or there is no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next time step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually turn the light for road A green; and when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually turn the light for road B green.

More specifically, the system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car's presence. If a car is present and the corresponding light is not green, the system moves to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. This pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system eventually responds to the presence of cars on both roads.
