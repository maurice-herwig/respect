The **Minepump** system manages a pump in response to environmental inputs from water and methane sensors. Here’s how it operates:

### **Inputs and Outputs**
- **Environment-controlled inputs**:
  - `highwater` (boolean): Indicates whether the water level is critically high.
  - `methane` (boolean): Indicates whether dangerous methane levels are detected.

- **System-controlled output**:
  - `pump` (boolean): Controls whether the pump is active (`true`) or inactive (`false`).

---

### **Assumptions on the Environment**
The environment behaves under the following constraints:
1. **Pump drainage effect**: If the pump runs continuously for two consecutive steps, the water level will no longer be critically high in the next step.
2. **Mutual exclusivity of hazards (immediate)**: If the pump is active, the environment cannot simultaneously have high water **and** methane. At least one of these conditions must be false.
3. **Mutual exclusivity of hazards (persistent methane)**: If methane was detected in the previous step, the environment will not allow both high water **and** methane in the current step (again, at least one must be false).

---

### **System Requirements (Guarantees)**
The system must respond as follows:

1. **Pump activation for high water**:
   - Whenever the `highwater` sensor detects a critical level, the system must activate the pump (`pump = true`) in the next step.

2. **Pump deactivation for methane**:
   - Whenever methane is detected (`methane = true`), the system must immediately turn off the pump (`pump = false`) in the next step to avoid ignition risks.

---

### **Summary of Behavior**
The pump operates under two conflicting priorities:
- **Water control**: It must drain water when levels rise (activating when `highwater` is detected).
- **Safety override**: It must shut down when methane is present (deactivating when `methane` is detected).

The environment ensures that these hazards (`highwater` and `methane`) cannot persist indefinitely together, nor can the pump run indefinitely without reducing water levels. The system’s decisions depend entirely on the latest sensor inputs, reacting in the next step.
