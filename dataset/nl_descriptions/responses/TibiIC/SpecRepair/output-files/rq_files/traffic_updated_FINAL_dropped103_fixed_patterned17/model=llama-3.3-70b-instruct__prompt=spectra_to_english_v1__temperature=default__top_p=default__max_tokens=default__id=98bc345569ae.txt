The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, represented by `cara` and `carb`, and an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that the emergency signal is usually false. Additionally, when the emergency signal is true, the environment is assumed to behave in specific ways: if a car is not present on road A, it will either not be present in the next state or the light will be green for road A, unless the emergency signal remains true; similarly, if a car is not present on road B, it will either not be present in the next state or the light will be green for road B, unless the emergency signal remains true. Furthermore, when the emergency signal is true and the light is green for a road, the car on that road will move, meaning it will not be present in the next state, unless the emergency signal remains true or the light is no longer green.

The system guarantees several behaviors. The traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the light green for road A, and similarly for road B. When the emergency signal is true, both lights will be red. 

The system starts in an initial state where it is prepared to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system's response to the presence of a car on a road follows a specific pattern: if a car is present and the corresponding light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green; once the light is green, the system will return to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern repeats indefinitely, ensuring that the system responds to the presence of cars on the roads in a timely manner.
