The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal from the environment. The environment controls the presence of cars A and B, indicated by the boolean variables carA and carB, and the emergency signal, a boolean variable emergency. The system controls the traffic lights for cars A and B, indicated by the boolean variables greenA and greenB.

The environment is assumed to behave in certain ways. Specifically, it is assumed that the emergency signal is not always present, meaning there will be times when the emergency signal is false. When a car is present and its corresponding light is red, the car will remain present at the next time step. However, when a car is present, its corresponding light is green, and there is no emergency, the car will move and no longer be present at the next time step. The same movement rule applies to car B when its light is green, regardless of the emergency signal.

The system guarantees certain behavior. The traffic lights for cars A and B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same response is guaranteed for car B. Additionally, when the emergency signal is present, both traffic lights will be red.

The system's response to the presence of cars A and B can be described by a pattern where the system initially is in a state where it can respond to the car's presence. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is green when the car is present. If the car is present and its light is not green, the system moves to a state where it must turn the light green. Once the light is green, the system returns to its initial state, allowing it to respond to the car's presence again. This pattern ensures that the system will always eventually respond to the presence of cars A and B by turning their corresponding lights green.
