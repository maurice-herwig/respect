The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two cars, A and B, in response to their presence and an emergency signal. The environment controls the presence of car A, car B, and the emergency signal. The system controls the green lights for car A and car B.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is eventually always the case that there is no emergency. Additionally, when car A is present and its light is red while car B's light is green, car A will remain present in the next state. Similarly, when car B is present and its light is red while car A's light is green, car B will remain present in the next state. Furthermore, when car A's light is green and car A is present, car A will move and not be present in the next state. The same applies to car B.

The system guarantees that the lights for car A and car B are never both red at the same time. When car A is present, the system will eventually turn its light green. The same applies to car B. If an emergency occurs, the system will turn both lights red. 

The system's response to the presence of a car is defined by a pattern that ensures the system will eventually turn the corresponding light green when the car is present. This pattern works by maintaining an internal state that tracks whether the system is currently responding to the car's presence. Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car's presence. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or its light is green. If the car is present and its light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is responding to the car's presence. The system remains in this state until the car's light is turned green, at which point it returns to the initial state. The system will infinitely often be in the initial state, ensuring that it will repeatedly respond to the car's presence.
