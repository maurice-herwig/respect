The **mine pump system** monitors and responds to two environmental conditions—water level and methane presence—while controlling a pump to manage water accumulation. The behavior is defined as follows:

**Environment-controlled inputs:**
- `highwater`: A signal indicating whether the water level has reached a critical high point.
- `methane`: A signal indicating whether dangerous levels of methane are detected.

**System-controlled output:**
- `pump`: A signal that activates or deactivates the pump to remove water.

**Assumptions on the environment:**
- Whenever the `highwater` signal is active (indicating critical water levels), the environment ensures that either:
  - The `highwater` signal will deactivate in the next step, or
  - The system keeps the `pump` off (`pump = false`).

  This implies that the environment does not allow both `highwater` to remain active *and* the pump to run indefinitely without resolution.

**Guarantees provided by the system:**
- **Safety guarantee (methane-based pump control):**
  - If methane is detected (`methane = true`), the system ensures the `pump` will turn *on* in the next step.
  - If methane is *not* detected (`methane = false`), the system ensures the `pump` will turn *off* in the next step.

  Together, these ensure the pump responds immediately to methane presence, reducing explosion risks.

**Initial conditions:**
The specification does not define explicit initial constraints, so the system’s initial behavior is unconstrained beyond the guarantees and assumptions described.
