The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

Additionally, when an emergency occurs, if a car is present on road A, it will move (i.e., its presence will be false in the next state) unless the light is not green for road A, and similarly for road B.

Second, if a car is present on road A and the light for road B is red, then the light for road A will be green. Third, if a car is present on road B and the light for road B is green, then this situation will persist. Finally, if there is no emergency, then both lights will not be red at the same time.

In terms of responsiveness, the system ensures that the presence of a car on a road will eventually lead to a green light for that road under certain conditions. Specifically, if a car is present on road A, then the system will respond by setting the light for road A to green when the light for road B is red. Similarly, if a car is present on road B, then the system will respond by setting the light for road B to green when it is already green.

The system's initial state has both lights red, and it will always eventually return to this state. The system's behavior is characterized by a set of safety and liveness conditions that ensure the traffic lights respond correctly to the presence of cars and emergencies.
