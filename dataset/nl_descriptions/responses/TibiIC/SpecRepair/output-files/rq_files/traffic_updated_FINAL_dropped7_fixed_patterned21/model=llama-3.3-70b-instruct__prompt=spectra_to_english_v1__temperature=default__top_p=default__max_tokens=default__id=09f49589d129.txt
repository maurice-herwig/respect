The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and whether there is an emergency situation (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green for roads A and B (`greena` and `greenb`).

The system must always ensure that at least one of the traffic lights is red, meaning that either the light for road A or the light for road B (or both) is not green at any given time.

When a car is present on road A, the system must eventually set the traffic light for road A to green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system must eventually set the traffic light for road B to green.

However, during an emergency, the system must keep both traffic lights red, as indicated by the requirement that the emergency situation must always be false for the traffic lights to be green.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: 

- When the traffic light for road A is red during an emergency, the car on road A will not move until either the emergency ends, the traffic light turns green, or the car on road A moves away.
- When the traffic light for road B is red during an emergency, the car on road B will not move until either the emergency ends, the traffic light turns green, or there is no car on road B.
- During an emergency, if the traffic light for road A is green, the car on road A will move away until either the emergency ends or the traffic light turns red.
- The traffic light for road B must be red during an emergency.

The system's response to the presence of cars on the roads follows a specific pattern: initially, the system is in a state where it can respond to the presence of a car by setting the corresponding traffic light to green. If a car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system moves to a state where it must set the traffic light to green. Once the traffic light is green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system always responds to the presence of cars on the roads by eventually setting the corresponding traffic lights to green.
