The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as the occurrence of emergency situations, denoted by `emergency`. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, denoted by `greena` and `greenb`, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: it will not have emergencies frequently, meaning that the emergency signal will be false most of the time. When the traffic light for road A is red, the environment will ensure that there is no car on road A or an emergency is occurring. Similarly, when the traffic light for road B is red, the environment will ensure that either an emergency is occurring or the traffic light for road B will turn green. The environment also ensures that when the traffic light for road A is green, any car on road A will move, and when the traffic light for road B is green, any car on road B will move unless an emergency is occurring.

The system guarantees the following behaviors: when there is no emergency, the traffic light for road B will not be green. When there is a car on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. When there is a car on road B, the system will eventually turn either the traffic light for road A or road B green. During an emergency, both traffic lights will be red. 

Initially, the system starts in a state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system will always be in one of two states: waiting for a car to arrive or waiting for the traffic light to turn green after a car has arrived. If a car arrives when the system is waiting for a car, it will turn the corresponding traffic light green if possible. If the traffic light is already green when a car arrives, the system will remain in the same state. The system will periodically return to the waiting state, ensuring that it can respond to new cars arriving on the roads.
