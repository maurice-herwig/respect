The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, an emergency situation, and police intervention. The system controls the state of a traffic light, specifically whether it is green. 

The environment is assumed to behave in two specific ways. First, when the police are not present, the car will either be present or an emergency situation will occur, or the car will not be present, at the next time step. Second, if a car is present, it will either not move or the traffic light will not be green at the next time step.

Given these assumptions, the system guarantees that it will often respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green. This guarantee is formalized through a pattern that describes the system's behavior in terms of its response to the car's presence. 

Initially, the system is in a state where it is not responding to the car. The system remains in this state as long as the car is not present or the traffic light is green when the car is present. If the car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the traffic light to turn green. Once the traffic light turns green, the system returns to its initial state. If the traffic light does not turn green while the system is in the waiting state, the system remains in that state. The system will infinitely often return to its initial state. 

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of a car, an emergency situation, and police intervention. The system-controlled output is the state of the traffic light, specifically whether it is green.
