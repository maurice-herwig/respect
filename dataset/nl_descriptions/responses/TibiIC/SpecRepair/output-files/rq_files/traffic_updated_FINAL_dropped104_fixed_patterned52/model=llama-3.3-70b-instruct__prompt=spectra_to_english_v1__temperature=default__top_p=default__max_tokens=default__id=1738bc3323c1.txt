The TrafficE2 system controls traffic flow based on input from the environment. The environment provides signals indicating the presence of cars A and B, as well as an emergency status. The system controls the green light indicators for cars A and B.

The environment-controlled inputs are the presence of car A, the presence of car B, and the emergency status. The system-controlled outputs are the green light indicators for cars A and B.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when the emergency status is true, car A will not be present in the next state unless the green light for car A is also true, or the emergency status remains true. Similarly, when the emergency status is true, car B will not be present in the next state unless the green light for car B is true, the emergency status remains true, or car B is not present. Additionally, when the emergency status is true, if car A is present, it will move and not be present in the next state unless the green light for car A is false or the emergency status remains true. Car B is also expected to move when the emergency status is true, and not be present in the next state unless the green light for car B is false.

The system guarantees that when car A is present, the green light for car A will eventually be true, and when car B is present, the green light for car B will eventually be true. This guarantee is achieved through a state machine that ensures the system responds to the presence of cars A and B by setting the corresponding green lights. The system starts in an initial state where it is ready to respond to the presence of cars. If a car is present and its corresponding green light is not set, the system transitions to a state where it awaits the setting of the green light. Once the green light is set, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next presence of a car. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial state, ready to respond to the presence of cars.
