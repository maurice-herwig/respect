The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B. The environment provides inputs indicating the presence of cars on roads A and B (`cara` and `carb`) and an emergency status (`emergency`). The system controls the traffic lights, setting them to green for roads A and B (`greena` and `greenb`).

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when there is an emergency, if the light for road A is red, there will be no car on road A; when there is an emergency, the light for road B will be green; when there is an emergency or no car on road A, the car on road A will move or the light will turn red at the next step; and when there is an emergency or no car on road B, the car on road B will move at the next step.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time; when there is a car on road A, the system will eventually set the light for road A to green; and when there is a car on road B, the system will eventually set the light for road B to green.

Initially, the system starts in a state where it is prepared to respond to the presence of cars on the roads. The system ensures that if a car is present on a road, the corresponding light will be set to green, and it will remain in this state until the car is gone or the light is turned red. If a car is present and the light is not green, the system will transition to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green, and it will stay in this state until the light turns green. The system will infinitely often return to the initial state where it is prepared to respond to the presence of cars on the roads.
