The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights at an intersection with two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, denoted by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as the occurrence of emergencies. The system controls the traffic lights for roads A and B, denoted by `greena` and `greenb`, respectively.

The environment is assumed to not have emergencies often, meaning that it is generally not in an emergency state. Additionally, when an emergency occurs, cars on road A are assumed to either not be present, become present at the next time step, or the traffic light for road A is green. Similar assumptions are made for cars on road B. Furthermore, when an emergency occurs and the traffic light for a road is green, cars on that road are assumed to either not be present or become absent at the next time step.

The system guarantees that the traffic lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the traffic light for road A green. The same guarantee is made for road B. During an emergency, the system ensures that both traffic lights are red. 

The system's behavior is also guided by a pattern that ensures responsiveness to the presence of cars. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to cars. The system remains in this state as long as no car is present or a car is present and the corresponding traffic light is green. If a car is present and its traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it must turn the traffic light green. Once the traffic light is turned green, the system returns to its initial state, ready to respond to the next car. This pattern ensures that the system periodically returns to a state where it is ready to respond to cars, guaranteeing liveness.
