The TrafficE2 system interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency situations, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways. It is assumed that the police will not be present indefinitely, and there will be times when they are not present. Additionally, when the traffic light is not green, a car will not start moving unless there is an emergency or the car is already moving. The environment is also assumed to ensure that when the emergency signal is about to be raised, the car will either stop moving or already be stopped, unless the traffic light is not green. A related assumption is that if there was no emergency in the previous moment, the car will not start moving unless it is already moving or the traffic light is not green.

The system guarantees certain behaviors. The traffic light will turn green periodically, and this will happen often enough that the system will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the light green. Furthermore, the system ensures that there will be times when there are no cars present.

The system's response to the presence of a car is defined by a specific pattern. Initially, the system is in a state where it is ready to respond to the car's presence. The system then enters a cycle where it remains in this ready state as long as there is no car present or the light is green when a car is present. If a car appears when the light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to the ready state. This cycle repeats, ensuring that the system periodically turns the light green in response to the car's presence.
