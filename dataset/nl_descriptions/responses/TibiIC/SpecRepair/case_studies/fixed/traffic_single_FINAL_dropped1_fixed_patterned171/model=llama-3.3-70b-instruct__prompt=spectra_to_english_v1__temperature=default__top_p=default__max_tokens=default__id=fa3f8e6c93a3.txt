The TrafficE2 system is a reactive system that interacts with its environment, which controls the presence of a car, emergency conditions, and police presence. The system controls the state of a traffic light, indicated by the green signal.

The environment is expected to behave in certain ways: it should not have police presence indefinitely, and when there is no emergency, the traffic light should be green or there should be no car. Additionally, when the police are present, the car should either not be moving in the next state or the traffic light should not be green.

The system guarantees that it will respond to the presence of a car by eventually turning the traffic light green when a car is present. This response is defined by a pattern that describes the system's behavior in terms of its response to the car's presence and the traffic light's state. The system starts in an initial state where it is ready to respond to the car's presence. 

The system's behavior is constrained by safety requirements that dictate how it transitions between states based on the car's presence and the traffic light's state. Specifically, when the system is in its initial state, it remains in that state if there is no car or if the car is present and the traffic light is green. If the car is present and the traffic light is not green, the system transitions to a different state. From this different state, the system returns to its initial state if the traffic light becomes green; otherwise, it remains in that state.

The system also ensures that it will infinitely often be in its initial state, which means it will periodically return to a state where it is ready to respond to the car's presence.
