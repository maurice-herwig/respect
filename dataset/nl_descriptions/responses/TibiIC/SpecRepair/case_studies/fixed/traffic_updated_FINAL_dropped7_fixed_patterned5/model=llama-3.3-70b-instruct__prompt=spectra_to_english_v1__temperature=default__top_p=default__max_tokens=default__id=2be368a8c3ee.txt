The TrafficE2 system controls traffic lights for two roads, A and B, in response to the presence of cars on these roads and emergency situations. The environment controls the presence of cars on roads A and B, indicated by `cara` and `carb`, respectively, as well as an emergency signal. The system controls the traffic lights, with `greena` and `greenb` indicating whether the lights are green for roads A and B, respectively.

The environment is assumed to behave in the following ways: when an emergency is in effect, if a car is not present on road A, the car will either become present, the emergency will continue, or the light for road A will be green. Similarly, for road B, when an emergency is in effect, if a car is not present, the car will either become present, the emergency will continue, the light for road B will be green, or there will be no car on road B. Additionally, when an emergency is in effect, if the light for road A is green, the car on road A will either move (become absent), the emergency will continue, or the light for road A will not be green. For road B, when an emergency is in effect, the light for road B will not be green.

The system guarantees the following behavior: the lights for roads A and B are never both red at the same time. When a car is present on road A, the system will eventually turn the light for road A green. Similarly, when a car is present on road B, the system will eventually turn the light for road B green. However, the system does not guarantee that an emergency will not occur.

The system's behavior is also constrained by the following rules: initially, the system is in a state where it is waiting for a car to arrive. If a car arrives on a road and the corresponding light is not green, the system transitions to a state where it is waiting for the light to turn green. Once the light turns green, the system returns to its initial state, waiting for the next car to arrive. The system ensures that it will infinitely often be in this initial waiting state.
