The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the levels of high water and methane, while the system controls the operation of a pump.

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. Correspondingly, the system starts with the pump turned off.

The system is required to behave in the following manner: if high water is present, then either methane is already present or the pump must be turned on at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, the pump must be turned off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is still on, then the high water level will be lowered at the next opportunity. Furthermore, if there is no methane, then either the high water level is already low or it will be lowered if methane is not present.

In summary, the system must respond to the presence of high water and methane by controlling the pump, while the environment is expected to follow certain rules regarding the levels of high water and methane. The system's primary goal is to ensure safe operation by turning the pump on or off based on the environmental conditions, and the environment's behavior is constrained to ensure the system can operate effectively.
