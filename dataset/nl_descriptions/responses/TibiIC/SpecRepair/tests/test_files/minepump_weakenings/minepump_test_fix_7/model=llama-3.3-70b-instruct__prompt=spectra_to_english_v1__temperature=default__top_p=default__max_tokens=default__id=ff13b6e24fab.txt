The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the high water and methane conditions, while the system controls the pump.

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. Under these initial conditions, the system guarantees that the pump is off.

The system is required to behave in the following way: if high water is present, the system must turn on the pump at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, the system must either turn off the pump at the next opportunity if high water is present, or ensure that the pump is off if high water is not present.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is still on, the high water condition will be resolved at the next opportunity. Furthermore, the environment is assumed to ensure that if there is no methane, either there is no high water or the absence of methane persists.

In summary, the system must respond to the presence of high water by turning on the pump, and respond to the presence of methane by turning off the pump if high water is present. The environment must ensure that the high water condition is resolved when the pump is on, and that the absence of methane is maintained if there is no high water.
