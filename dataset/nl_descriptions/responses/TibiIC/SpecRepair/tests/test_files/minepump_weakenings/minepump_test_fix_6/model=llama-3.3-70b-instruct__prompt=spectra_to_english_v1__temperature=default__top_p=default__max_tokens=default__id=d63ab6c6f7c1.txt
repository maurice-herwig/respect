The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: the presence of high water and the presence of methane. The environment controls the high water and methane conditions, while the system controls the pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. Correspondingly, the system starts with the pump turned off.

The system is required to behave in the following way: if high water is present, then the pump must be off, but it is allowed to turn on at the next step. If methane is detected, the pump must be turned off at the next step.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump was previously on and is currently on, then at the next step, there will be no high water. Additionally, it is assumed that if the pump is on, then either there is no high water or there is no methane.

In summary, the system must control the pump based on the environmental conditions of high water and methane, while the environment's behavior is restricted by assumptions regarding the relationships between the pump's state and these conditions.
