The Minepump system operates in an environment where two conditions are monitored: high water levels and methane presence. The environment controls the detection of high water and methane, while the system controls the operation of a pump. 

Initially, it is assumed that there is no high water and no methane present. In this initial state, the pump is not operating.

The system is required to operate as follows: if high water is detected, then the pump must be operating either immediately or at the next opportunity. Additionally, if methane is detected, then the pump must be turned off at the next opportunity.

The environment is assumed to behave in a way that if the pump has been operating and continues to operate, then the high water level will be reduced at the next opportunity. Furthermore, the environment is assumed to ensure that if the pump is not operating, then either there is no high water or there is no methane.

In summary, the system must respond to high water by operating the pump and to methane by stopping the pump, under the assumption that the environment will reduce high water levels when the pump operates continuously and will not have both high water and methane when the pump is not operating.
