This specification describes a simple traffic-light control system for an intersection with two directions, A and B. The system controls which direction has a green light, while the environment represents the presence or absence of cars approaching from each direction.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- The environment provides two inputs: `carA` and `carB`, which indicate whether a car is present at directions A and B, respectively.
- The system produces two outputs: `greenA` and `greenB`, which indicate whether the light for directions A and B is green.

**Assumptions on the Environment:**
1. The environment ensures that once a car appears at a direction (*A* or *B*), it will not disappear as long as the light for that direction is not green. In other words, a car remains at direction *A* until the light turns green for *A*, and similarly for *B*.
2. The environment guarantees that cars will not simultaneously approach from both directions *A* and *B* infinitely often. That is, there are only finitely many instances where cars appear at both directions at the same time.

**System Guarantees:**
1. **Safety Requirement:** The system ensures that the lights for directions *A* and *B* are never green at the same time; at most one direction may have a green light.
2. **Response to Cars:**
   - If a car is present at direction *A*, the system must eventually turn the light green for direction *A*.
   - If a car is present at direction *B* and no car is present at direction *A*, the system must eventually turn the light green for direction *B*.

The specification does not explicitly define initial conditions for the traffic lights. The system must satisfy these guarantees starting from the beginning of operation.
